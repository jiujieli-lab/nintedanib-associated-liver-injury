Preface
This guide has been prepared speciﬁcally for people who edit ACS Style
manuscripts that will be published by MDPI. The guide has been divided into
two parts as follows:
Part I: In-text citations.
The citation format for all the references mentioned in the main text.
Part II: The reference list.
Provides the information necessary for a reader to locate and retrieve
any source you cite in the body of the paper. Each source you cite in the
paper must appear in your reference list; likewise, each entry in the
reference list must be cited in your text.
MDPI’s style for citations and references lists is widely based on the style used
by the American Chemical Society. Please refer to the ACS Style Guide [1] or
enquire with a journal’s Editorial Oﬃce if you have any questions regarding the
preparation of citations and reference lists.

Part I. In-Text Citations
1. Citations
•

The references should be cited using Arabic numbers within square brackets, “[]”, such as “[1]”,
“[1,2]”, and “[2–4]”. Additionally, they must be cited in ascending order of appearance in the text.

•

We recommend preparing the references with a bibliography software package, such as EndNote,
ReferenceManager, or Zotero, to avoid typing mistakes and duplicated references.

•

The Ref. number should not be used as the subject in sentences of the main text (e.g., “[1]
introduced …” is incorrect). We advise adding “Ref.” or “author names” before the citation, e.g.,
replace “[1] proposes a systematic” with “Ref. [1] proposes a systematic” or “Liu [1] proposes a
systematic”.

•

Citations with more than two consecutive references should utilize an en dash (–) in between the
numbers: “[1]”, “[1,2]”, “[3–5]”, and “[1,3–5,7]”.

•

Other content, such as page number, text, etc., should not be included in the square brackets of
the reference citations; please remove them. For example, “[14, pp. 223–224]” should be replaced
by “(pp. 223–224, [14])” or “[14] (pp. 223–224)”, or the page range should be included in the
bibliography; the formats below are also acceptable:
[58] p. 220
[58], p. 220

Part II. Reference List
2. Reference List
2.1. Reference List Format
•

Section Heading should be correct; it can be “Reference(s)” or “Reference(s) and Note(s)”,
depending on the content.

•

Use Arabic numbers with a period, such as “1.”, “2.”, “3.”… A tab should be added after each
reference number.

•

Alignment = justiﬁed; hanging = 0.75 cm; font size = 9 pt; line spacing: at least 14 pt.

2.2. General Considerations
To allow automated treatment of bibliographic information, references and citations must
be prepared according to the following general rules:
•

Only one reference should be listed per single number, e.g., “1. (a) ref. 1; (b) ref. 2” is not allowed;
it should be divided into two references.

•

Duplicate references are not allowed.

•

Include the digital object identiﬁer (DOI) for all references where available.

•

Avoid including notes with the references, e.g., do not write:
10. Díaz et al. recently reported a multigram display of azide and cyanide components on a
versatile scaﬀold, see: Díaz, D.D.; Converso, A.; Sharpless, K.B.; Finn, M.G. 2,6-Dichloro-9thiabicyclo[3.3.1]nonane: Multigram Display of Azide and Cyanide Components on a Versatile
Scaﬀold. Molecules 2006, 11, 212–218.
Instead, format the reference as follows:
10. Díaz, D.D.; Converso, A.; Sharpless, K.B.; Finn, M.G. 2,6-Dichloro-9-thiabicyclo[3.3.1]nonane:
Multigram Display of Azide and Cyanide Components on a Versatile Scaﬀold. Molecules 2006, 11,
212–218, https://doi.org/10.3390/11040212.

And add the note in the text next to the citation:
… recently reported by Díaz et al. [10].

3. Reference Elements
3.1. Author Element
•

Authors must be listed in the same order as they appear in the original document. Diﬀerent
authors are separated with semicolons (;):
Author 1; Author 2; Author 3

•

For every author, list the last name ﬁrst, then the ﬁrst letter of the ﬁrst name and, if available,
the ﬁrst letter of the middle name.

•

Last names should be separated from the ﬁrst and middle name by a comma, and the ﬁrst
and middle initials should end with a period:
Lastname, F.M.; Lastname, F.M.

•

Special cases include “van”, “von”, “de”, and others:
von Lastname, F.M.; van Lastname, F.M.

•

For author names with suﬃxes “Jr.”, “Sr.”, “II”, etc.:
Lastname, F.M., Jr.; Lastname, F.M., Sr.

•

For documents co-authored by a large number of persons (more than 10 authors), you can cite the
first ten authors, then add a semicolon and add ‘et al.’ at the end:
Author 1; Author 2; Author 3; Author 4; Author 5; Author 6; Author 7; Author 8; Author 9; Author
10; et al.

3.2. Editor Element
•

List editors’ names in the same way as authors’ names, except that multiple editors are separated
by commas instead of semicolons:
Editor 1, Editor 2, Editor 3

•

Add ‘Ed.’ (one editor) or ‘Eds.’ (multiple editors) at the end of the list:
Editor 1, Ed.

or
Editor 1, Editor 2, Editor 3, Eds.

4. Common Types of References
4.1. Journal/Periodical Articles
Basic Format:
Author 1, F.M.; Author 2, F.M.; Author 3, F.M. Title of the article. Journal Abbreviation Year, Volume,
Firstpage–Lastpage/Article Number. https://doi.org/.
Example: 1:
Díaz, D.D.; Converso, A.; Sharpless, K.B.; Finn, M.G. 2,6-Dichloro-9-thiabicyclo[3.3.1]nonane:
Multigram Display of Azide and Cyanide Components on a Versatile Scaﬀold. Molecules 2006, 11,
212–218, https://doi.org/10.3390/11040212.

Example: 2: Journal article with reprinted work
Rindler, W. Visual horizons in world-models. Mon. Not. R. Astr. Soc. 1956, 116, 662–677. Reprint in
Gen. Rel. Grav. 2002, 34, 133–153.
Example: 3: Journal article with correction work
The type of correction is determined by the actual type of the article and may include, but is not
limited to “Correction in”, “Erratum in”, “Addendum in”, and “Corrigendum in”.
Malik, R.; Chauhan, G.; Traylor, M.; Sargurupremraj, M.; Okada, Y.; Mishra, A.; Rutten-Jacobs, L.;
Giese, A.-K.; van der Laan, S.W.; Gretarsdottir, S.; et al. Multiancestry genome-wide association study
of 520,000 subjects identiﬁes 32 loci associated with stroke and stroke subtypes. Nat. Genet. 2018, 50,
524–537. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-018-0058-3. Correction in Nat. Genet. 2019, 51, 1192–1193.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-019-0449-0.
Example: 4: Retraction work
If it is necessary to cite a retracted article, the following reference format should be used:
Morisky, D.E.; Ang, A.; Krousel-Wood, M.; Ward, H.J. Predictive validity of a medication adherence
measure in an outpatient setting. J. Clin. Hypertens. 2008, 10, 348–354. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.17517176.2008.07572.x. Retraction in J. Clin. Hypertens. 2023, 25, 889.
Tips:
•

Journal abbreviations are listed at: http://www.issn.org/2-22661-LTWA-online.php; do not
abbreviate journal names which only have one word (e.g., Energies, Humanities, Religions, etc.;
keep the journal name Physical A or Physical B as it is).

•

The issue number is not included. (The issue part number, e.g., Pt 2 or Supplemental Issue
Number (Suppl. S36), should also not be included).

4.2. Books
4.2.1. Books Without Editors
Basic Format:
Author 1, F.M.; Author 2, F.M.; Author 3, F.M. Title of the Book, Edition (if available); Publisher:
City, Country, Year; Chapter (optional), pp. Page range (optional).
Example:
Desiraju, G.R.; Steiner, T. The Weak Hydrogen Bond in Structural Chemistry and Biology, 2nd ed.;
Oxford University Press: New York, NY, USA, 1999; pp. 10–25.
4.2.2. Books with Editors
Basic Format:
Author 1, F.M.; Author 2, F.M.; Author 3, F.M. Title of the contribution. In Title of the Book, Edition
(if available); Editor 1, Editor 2, Eds.; Publisher: City, Country, Year; Volume (optional), pp. Pages
(optional).
Example:
Dodd, J.S.; Solla, L.; Bérard, P.M. References. In The ACS Style Guide, 3rd ed.; Coghill, A., Garson,
L., Eds.; American Chemical Society: Washington, DC, USA, 2006; Volume 1, pp. 292–325.
Tips:
•

Pagination: Use the abbreviations “p.” and “pp.” to indicate single and multiple pages,
respectively, e.g.:
p. 1

•

Editor: Note that multiple editors are separated by commas (unlike authors, which are separated

•

Publisher Location: Use the city name where the publisher’s headquarters are located or, if a
subsidiary publishes the book, use the subsidiary’s city name; for cities in the USA and Canada,
add the city name, the abbreviation of the state, and ‘USA’, e.g., Springﬁeld, IL, USA.

4.3. Conferences or Meetings
4.3.1. Proceedings
Basic Format:
Author 1, F.M.; Author 2, F.M.; Author 3, F.M. Title of Presentation. In Proceedings of the Name
of the Conference, Location of Conference, Country, Date of Conference (Day Month Year);
Abstract Number (optional), Pagination (optional).
Example:
Chum, O.; Philbin, J.; Zisserman, A. Near duplicate image detection: Min-Hash and tf-idf
weighting. In Proceedings of the 19th British Machine Vision Conference (BMVC 2008), Leeds,
UK, 1–4 September 2008; pp. 812–815.
4.3.2. Oral Presentations Without Published Material
Basic Format:
Author 1, F.M.; Author 2, F.M.; Author 3, F.M. Title of Presentation (if any). Presented at the
Name of Conference, Location of Conference, Country, Date of Conference; Paper number (if
available).
Example:
Zhang, Z.; Chen, H.; Zhong, J.; Chen, Y.; Lu, Y. ZnO Nanotip-based QCM Biosensors. Presented
at the IEEE International Frequency Control Symposium and Exposition, Miami, FL, USA, 4–7
June 2006.
4.3.3. Conference Proceedings Published in a Book
Basic Format:
Author 1, F.M.; Author 2, F.M.; Author 3, F.M. Title of the Presentation. In Title of the Collected
Work; Editor 1, Editor 2, Eds.; Publisher: City, Country, Year; Abstract Number (optional),
Pagination (optional).
Example: 1
Boom, K.D.; Bower, M.; Arguel, A.; Siemon, J.; Scholkmann, A. Relationship between
computational thinking and a measure of intelligence as a general problem-solving ability. In
Proceedings of the Annual Conference on Innovation and Technology in Computer Science Education,
ITiCSE; ACM: New York, NY, USA, 2018; pp. 206–211.
Example: 2:
Nilsson, A.; Petersson, F.; Persson, H.W.; Jönsson, H.; Laurell, T. Manipulation of Suspended
Particles in a Laminar Flow. In Micro Total Analysis Systems 2002, Proceedings of the µTAS 2002
Symposium, Nara, Japan, 3−7 November 2002; Baba, Y., Shoji, S., van den Berg, A., Eds.; Kluwer
Academic: Dordrecht, The Netherlands, 2002; Volume 2, pp. 751−753.

4.3.4. Conference Proceedings Published in a Journal
Basic Format:
Author 1, F.M.; Author 2, F.M.; Author 3, F.M. Title of the Presentation. Journal Abbreviation Year,
Volume, Firstpage–Lastpage/Article Number.
Example:
Culot, C.; Durant, F.; Mosley, D.H.; Andre, J.M.; Vercauteren, D.P. Conformational Analyses of
the Polymorphism of Triglycerides. Int. J. Quantum Chem. Quantum Biol. Symp. 1994, 52, 57−77.
https://doi.org/10.1002/qua.560520706.
4.4. Website and Online Resources
Basic Format:
Author (if available). Title of the Webpage (if available). Available online: http://... (accessed on
date).
Example:
Choosing Wisely Canada. Sixteen Tests and Treatments to Question in Orthopaedics. 2023.
Available online: https://choosingwiselycanada.org/recommendation/orthopaedics/ (accessed on
12 August 2023).
4.5. Thesis
Basic Format:
Author, F.M. Title of Thesis. Level of Thesis, Degree-Granting University, Location of University,
Date (Year, Day Month are optional).
Example:
Mäckel, H. Capturing the Spectra of Silicon Solar Cells. Ph.D. Thesis, The Australian National
University, Acton, Australia, December 2004.
4.6. Patent
Basic Format:
Patent Owner 1; Patent Owner 2; et al. Title of Patent. Patent Number, Date (Day Month Year, the
Application granted date).
Example:
Sheem, S.K. Low-Cost Fiber Optic Pressure Sensor. U.S. Patent 6,738,537, 18 May 2004.
Thomas, W.M.; Nicholas, E.D.; Needham, J.C.; Murch, M.G.; Temple-Smith, P.; Dawes, C.J.
Friction Stir Butt Welding. International Patent Application No. PCT/GB92/02203; GB Patent
Application No. 9125978.8; U.S. Patent Application No. 5,460,317, 6 December 1991.
4.7. Standard
Basic Format:
Standard’s Number; Standard’s Title. Publisher: City, Country, Year.
Example:
TB 10099-2017; Code for Design of Railway Station and Terminal. China Railway Publishing
House: Beijing, China, 2017.
4.8. Software
Basic Format:
Creator (if available). Title of the Software, version, revision or edition (if available); comments (if
available); Publisher: Place of Publication, Year.

4.9. Magazines and Newspapers
Basic Format:
Author 1, F.M.; Author 2, F.M.; Author 3, F.M. Title of article. Title of Periodical, Day Month Year,
Pagination (Optional).
Example:
Squires, S. Falling short on nutrients. The Washington Post, 4 October 2005, p. H1.
4.10. Reports and Bulletins
Basic Format:
Author 1, F.M.; Author 2, F.M.; Author 3, F.M. Title of Report or Bulletin; Technical Report or
Bulletin Number (Optional); Publisher: City, Country, Date; Pagination (Optional).
Example:
Hung, C.-C.; Hurst, J.; Santiago, D. Boron Nitride Nanoribbons from Exfoliation of Boron Nitride
Nanotubes; NASA/TM-2017-219455; National Aeronautics and Space Administration, Glenn
Research Center: Cleveland, OH, USA, 2017
