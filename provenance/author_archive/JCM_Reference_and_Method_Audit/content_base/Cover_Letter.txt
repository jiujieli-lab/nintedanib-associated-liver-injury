Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, “Clinical proteomics-guided network integration prioritizes PDGFRA and FLT4 for investigation of nintedanib-associated liver injury”, for publication as an Article in Journal of Clinical Medicine.

Nintedanib benefits patients with fibrosing interstitial lung disease, but hepatic adverse effects can interrupt treatment. We address the translational question of which receptor and liver-cell context should be investigated to improve hepatic tolerability while preserving pulmonary antifibrotic activity. The study connects comparative pharmacovigilance and a human serum injury-protein phenotype with biochemical pharmacology, cellular drug responses and donor-balanced liver networks.

PDGFRA and FLT4 remained the leading priorities across several integration rules and retained top-five positions in all 5000 source-weight perturbations. Published experimental measurements provide a biochemical basis for their engagement by nintedanib. Independent hepatic exposure identifies KDR as a pharmacodynamic comparator, while pulmonary datasets define relevant activity-preservation readouts. Matched-background and target-pair analyses delimit the interpretation of these priorities and guide receptor-specific perturbation and rescue experiments at controlled drug exposure.

The manuscript fits the journal’s clinical and translational scope by linking a treatment-limiting safety problem to measurable human injury proteins and a focused experimental strategy. It provides an integrated basis for selecting hepatic receptor contexts and pharmacodynamic comparators, with source-specific methods, complete supporting results and reproducibility materials.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
