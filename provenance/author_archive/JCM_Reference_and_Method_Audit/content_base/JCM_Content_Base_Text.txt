Clinical proteomics-guided network integration prioritizes PDGFRA and FLT4 for investigation of nintedanib-associated liver injury

Abstract

Background/Objectives: Liver injury can interrupt nintedanib treatment for fibrosing interstitial lung disease. We connected human injury proteins with drug pharmacology and liver-cell networks to prioritize receptors for mechanistic investigation.

Methods: We integrated active-comparator pharmacovigilance, 13 human serum proteins, biochemical and cellular drug responses, and a five-donor liver atlas. Donor-balanced networks, matched-background calibration, six aggregation algorithms, donor omission and weight perturbation evaluated candidate selection. Published binding measurements and single/joint network interventions characterized PDGFRA and FLT4. Independent hepatic profiles from 22 mice and pulmonary datasets assessed tissue context.

Results: PDGFRA and FLT4 ranked first and second under four aggregation rules and remained among the top five in all 5000 weight perturbations. PDGFRA remained first after donor omission; FLT4 ranked second to fifth. Reported dissociation constants were 16 and 95 nM, respectively. The narrow hepatic reporting odds ratio for nintedanib versus pirfenidone was 3.997 (95% confidence interval 3.226–4.951), with temporal heterogeneity. FBP1 distinguished drug-related from other acute liver injury (Cliff’s delta −0.505; q=0.000469). Competitive ranking and network interventions did not establish significant enrichment. Independent liver analysis identified decreased Kdr expression within the candidate family (log2 difference −0.280; q=0.0386; genome-wide q=0.119), without significant ranking–response association. Pulmonary responses varied by model.

Conclusions: PDGFRA and FLT4 are reproducible priorities for studying nintedanib-associated liver injury. Their biochemical engagement and cellular context support exposure-controlled perturbation and rescue experiments that measure hepatic injury while preserving pulmonary antifibrotic activity. KDR provides a hepatic pharmacodynamic comparator; the protective intervention direction remains to be established.

Keywords: Nintedanib; interstitial lung disease; drug-induced liver injury; clinical proteomics; virtual perturbation; donor-balanced networks; target prioritization; PDGFRA; FLT4

1. Introduction

Antifibrotic treatment must remain tolerable long enough to alter the course of progressive lung disease. Fibrosing interstitial lung diseases include idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and progressive phenotypes arising in autoimmune, hypersensitivity, and other disorders [1, 2]. Nintedanib reduced forced-vital-capacity decline in the INPULSIS, INBUILD, and SENSCIS trials, establishing its use across several of these populations [3, 4, 5]. The FIBRONEER trials and subsequent authorizations of nerandomilast have expanded therapeutic options [6, 7, 8, 9, 10]. Preserving the benefit of available treatments nevertheless requires understanding the biological processes that force dose reduction or withdrawal.

Liver injury is one such obstacle. Long-term and post-marketing studies describe interruptions and discontinuations during nintedanib treatment [11, 12, 13]. Its prescribing information includes serious and occasionally fatal drug-induced liver injury (DILI), with biochemical monitoring before treatment, during the first three months, and periodically thereafter [14]. Hepatic impairment increases exposure, and pharmacokinetic variability complicates the connection between prescribed dose and tissue response [15, 16]. Clinical assessment must also separate drug causation from competing liver diseases, concomitant exposures, and the natural course of acute injury [17, 18, 19]. Recent antifibrotic safety analyses reinforce the need to identify molecular strategies that improve hepatic tolerability while preserving treatment benefit [20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25].

Nintedanib's pharmacology offers a defined starting point. The drug inhibits vascular endothelial growth factor, fibroblast growth factor, and platelet-derived growth factor receptor families [26, 27]. Their effects depend on the tissue and cell type in which they are perturbed. Drug disposition adds a second source of variation: carboxylesterase-mediated hydrolysis to BIBF 1202 and subsequent glucuronidation contribute to clearance, while CYP3A4 can generate iminium-ion metabolites [28, 29]. The translational challenge is to identify which receptor and liver-cell context should be interrogated first, and which clinically grounded injury measurements should determine the outcome.

Public human serum proteomics supplies a clinically interpretable phenotype. Ravindra and colleagues reported candidate proteins measured in adjudicated DILI, healthy volunteers, and patients with acute non-drug liver injury [30]. Together with mechanistic biomarker research, these observations allow candidate pathways to be evaluated against measurable manifestations of hepatocellular damage [31]. Human liver single-cell data then place drug-binding proteins and injury-protein genes in their cellular context [32]. Toxicogenomic resources, LINCS, and chemical single-cell transcriptomics can test whether drug exposure perturbs related processes [33, 34, 35]. The resulting question is specific: which pharmacological interventions could alter the clinically observed injury phenotype in a biologically plausible liver compartment?

Answering this question computationally requires more than overlap among gene lists. Virtual knockout estimates network responses without performing a physical intervention [36], and recent benchmarks show that complex perturbation models do not necessarily outperform simple baselines [37, 38]. Numerical displacement, reference-donor composition, transcript detection, and calibration of the comparison background can each change which molecule appears most compelling. We therefore embedded continuous clinical protein effects within donor-balanced networks, evaluated the raw numerical scale before ranking virtual perturbations, and compared established covariance, propagation, and aggregation approaches on the same candidate set [39, 40, 41, 42, 43]. This clinically anchored integration links a human injury phenotype to receptor selection, tests the robustness of that selection, and specifies the cellular experiments needed to advance the leading candidates.

We followed one translational sequence: establish the hepatic safety question, define the human protein phenotype, connect it to drug pharmacology within liver cells, evaluate the reproducibility of candidate selection, and challenge the resulting priorities in independent drug-exposed liver. Because a clinically useful intervention must also preserve pulmonary benefit, we assessed lung response across public animal, single-cell, and protein datasets [44, 45, 46, 47]. Our objective was to convert these resources into a focused, reproducible set of receptor candidates and a coordinated perturbation-and-rescue strategy for improving the hepatic tolerability of antifibrotic treatment.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1. Study design and analysis sequence

We conducted a secondary analysis of public clinical, pharmacological and experimental data to connect nintedanib exposure with a human liver-injury protein phenotype and identify targets for subsequent perturbation-and-rescue experiments. The study sequence is summarized in Fig. 1A. The analysis followed observational-study reporting principles and READUS-PV recommendations where applicable [48, 49, 50]. Comparative pharmacovigilance defined the clinical problem; human serum proteins supplied the phenotype; drug-binding and exposure-response data positioned candidate targets within donor-balanced liver networks. Numerical calibration, alternative aggregation rules and biological-sample sensitivity analyses tested the resulting priorities. An independently identified nintedanib-exposed liver dataset challenged the fixed ranking, while pulmonary datasets evaluated the treatment response that a liver-sparing intervention would need to preserve. These measurements were integrated without treating circulating proteins as established drug targets or network proximity as a causal effect.

The candidate universe comprised 17 pharmacological proteins: ABL1, AXL, CDK4, FGFR1, FGFR2, FGFR3, FGFR4, FLT1, FLT3, FLT4, JAK1, KDR, LRRK2, LYN, MET, PDGFRA and TYK2. The clinical phenotype comprised the 13 proteins released by the serum-proteomics source study. All candidates and discordant findings were retained. Source-specific analyses were initially frozen on 4 September 2026. Additional numerical auditing, clinical-weighted projection, shrinkage-network estimation, aggregation comparisons and shared-reference sensitivities were developed on 19 September 2026. Their settings and the final integration rule were fixed before inspection of GSE125975. They were not prospectively preregistered. Dataset eligibility depended on exposure, tissue, comparison, downloadable measurements and recoverable experimental units, rather than agreement with a preferred mechanism. Tables 1 and 2 identify resources and provenance. Additional file 1 contains Extended Methods and detailed results; Additional file 2 contains the supplementary figures; Additional file 3 contains the complete supplementary tables. Supplementary Figures S1–S12 are provided in Additional file 2. Target-focused binding and virtual-intervention methods and results are detailed in Additional file 4, with their complete numerical tables in Additional file 5.

2.2. Comparative hepatic-event reporting

We queried openFDA drug/event, using the release updated 30 July 2026 and reports received from 15 October 2014 to 30 June 2026. The unit was the latest version of a unique safetyreportid. Exact normalized generic-name searches identified nintedanib and pirfenidone reports; reports containing both drugs were excluded from both groups. Pirfenidone supplied an active comparator used in the same clinical disease domain. The primary narrow endpoint contained ten hepatic preferred terms, including drug-induced liver injury, hepatotoxicity, toxic hepatitis and hepatic failure; the broad endpoint added 16 laboratory or hepatic-disorder terms. Complete custom term sets are documented in the analysis code. They are not licensed Standardised MedDRA Queries. An aminotransferase-plus-bilirubin/jaundice co-reporting pattern was exploratory and was not adjudicated Hy's law.

Reporting odds ratios (RORs) and proportional reporting ratios used the corresponding 2×2 report tables, with log-scale Wald 95% confidence intervals (CIs), Fisher exact tests and a 0.5 correction only for zero cells. A contrast with no endpoint reports in either arm was non-estimable. An active-comparator Bayesian information component used 100,000 Jeffreys–Dirichlet posterior draws; it was not represented as the WHO production BCPNN algorithm. Annual estimates, Tarone-adjusted Breslow–Day tests, Cochran's Q and I² evaluated temporal heterogeneity. Mantel–Haenszel estimates with Robins–Breslow–Greenland CIs and 30,000 within-year multinomial bootstrap replicates were descriptive stratified summaries when homogeneity failed. Exclusion of 2022 and calendar-period subdivision were post hoc influence analyses.

The complete specifications included alternative drug aliases, endpoint definitions, ILD indication phrases, report-level suspect-drug restrictions and demographic strata, following specification-curve transparency principles [51]. openFDA drugcharacterization codes were interpreted as 1=suspect, 2=concomitant and 3=interacting. Report-level searches flatten drug arrays and cannot link a role or indication to the indexed product. A same-drug-element indication audit was therefore reported among cases without calculating an ROR lacking corresponding denominators. Onset intervals required exact matching-drug start and report-level event dates and an interval of 0–365 days; descriptive Weibull fitting used days+0.5. These report-based analyses estimate disproportionality, not incidence or treatment effects. Frozen query logs, latest-version derived tables and checksums support audit; complete raw JSON responses require renewed retrieval from the original service.

2.3. Human serum protein phenotype

We reanalysed the source workbook from Ravindra et al. [30]. Discovery groups comprised healthy volunteers (HV; n=10), DILI onset and follow-up (DO and DF; n=10 each), non-drug acute liver injury onset and follow-up (NDO and NDF; n=5 each), and fatty-liver controls (n=10). The source-selected panel contained ACO1, ASS1, FAH, CPS1, ALDOB, HPD, OTC, DMGDH, GSTA1, FBP1, PCK2, CES1 and LECT2. Confirmatory source groups comprised HV (n=60), DO (n=82) and NDO (n=34). Actual denominators were protein- and contrast-specific; this was reanalysis of a released candidate panel, not unrestricted proteome discovery.

Non-detects remained missing in the primary analysis. Source-row counts, duplicated blocks, the starred PCK2 observation and the one-cell DMGDH-HV overflow were audited under the documented extraction rules. HPD was excluded from discovery-to-confirmatory replication counting because its discovery entries duplicated ALDOB. The workbook did not establish stable participant linkage across proteins or follow-up blocks. We consequently analysed proteins separately, used unpaired exploratory follow-up comparisons and did not construct a participant-level multivariable classifier.

Primary contrasts used two-sided Mann–Whitney tests and Cliff's delta with 2,000 group-stratified bootstrap percentile CIs [52]. Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) correction covered available proteins within cohort and contrast [53]. Apparent single-protein receiver-operating-characteristic areas under the curve were descriptive; they were not cross-validated performance estimates. Welch tests after protein-specific log transformation, half-minimum replacement of non-detects, detection-frequency tests and source-value exclusions supplied sensitivities. ALT correlations used only same-row protein–ALT pairs. Within-DO correlations were primary for severity association; pooled HV+DO correlations were contextual because case–control separation can inflate association. Continuous absolute clinical effect sizes supplied network seeds without selecting only statistically significant proteins.

The discovery measurements were variance-stabilizing-normalized tandem-mass-tag intensities; confirmatory source blocks were analysed on their reported protein-specific assay scales. We did not merge these scales into one pooled abundance matrix. For secondary log transformation, the offset was half the smallest positive value for the relevant protein. Bootstrap sampling preserved each comparison group. Follow-up measurements represented the observations actually released, without assuming nominal article-level totals or reconstructing participant pairing. Protein–ALT CIs also used 2,000 observation-level bootstrap resamples. Lower-limit ties were retained when explicitly supplied, while undocumented substitutions for non-detects in correlation panels were excluded from primary estimation.

2.4. Pharmacology and cellular drug-response features

ChEMBL records for nintedanib (CHEMBL502835) were restricted to exact-relation quantitative human single-protein activities with available pChEMBL, one mapped target component, no validity warning and no duplicate flag. Potency required maximum pChEMBL ≥6; multiple-assay and multiple-document support required at least two assays or independent documents, respectively. Heterogeneous biochemical activities supplied ranking information, not clinical exposure thresholds.

In GSE70138, the nintedanib perturbagen BRD-K49075727 had six HepG2 24-h Level 5 consensus signatures at 0.04, 0.12, 0.37, 1.11, 3.33 and 10 µM [34]. The 12,328-gene matrix contained 978 measured landmarks and 11,350 inferred genes. Consensus doses were not biological replicates. Spearman correlations with log10 concentration used all 720 dose-label permutations and genome-wide BH correction. Maximum absolute z score, normalized log-dose area under the curve and leave-one-dose-out direction stability characterized response. Competitive tests for the 13 clinical proteins, seven prespecified mechanism modules and three pharmacology sets used 25,000 random sets matched for landmark, best-inferred and other-inferred composition; statistics used within-stratum response percentiles. Module and pharmacology-set multiplicity families were corrected separately. MCF7 Sci-Plex signatures at 1 and 10 µM were compared descriptively with HepG2 at approximately matched doses [35]. Their truncated positive/negative gene lists made overlap-conditional concordance contextual; unreported genes were unavailable rather than non-responsive.

2.5. Target-focused biochemical binding evidence and molecular-property predictions

We further resolved the PDGFRA and FLT4 records within the ChEMBL extraction by endpoint, assay and source document. Direct dissociation constants (Kd), inhibition constants (Ki) and half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC50) were analysed separately. The specified eligibility rules defined the primary set. A labelled assay-format sensitivity additionally admitted exact, validity-clean human single-protein records whose descriptions specified recombinant kinase assays but whose BioAssay Ontology format was recorded generically. Duplicate-flagged, nonquantitative kinetic and single-concentration inhibition records were retained in the disposition table and excluded from potency summaries. We reported every eligible concentration, observed range, document-balanced median and leave-one-document-out median; matched-document contrasts compared the same endpoint without assuming matched assay conditions. Assay types were summarized separately without inferential pooling. Direct Kd observations were traced to the original kinase-binding study [54].

The connected Inductive Bio service predicted nintedanib LogD at pH 7.4 and acidic/basic pKa from the stereochemically specified neutral-parent ChEMBL SMILES. Resolved model versions were mcp_public_logd 1.7.0 and mcp_public_apka/mcp_public_bpka 1.4.0. The exact input, returned values, model configuration identifiers and applicability flags were archived. Provider-returned lower and upper bounds were retained without assigning an unreported confidence level. These physicochemical predictions describe the ligand and were not used as target-binding or molecular-dynamics measurements (Additional files 4 and 5).

2.6. Liver-cell localization and numerical perturbation calibration

The deposited normalized GSE115469 atlas contained 8,444 cells from five donors [32]. Published labels defined hepatocyte (n=3,501), endothelial (n=844), macrophage (n=1,192), other immune (n=2,751), cholangiocyte (n=119) and stellate (n=37) compartments. Mean expression and detection fraction were calculated within donor and compartment before equal-donor averaging. Cells did not supply independent biological degrees of freedom. The 37 stellate cells from four donors supported exploratory localization only. Network universes combined 650 variance-ranked eligible genes with forced clinical, pharmacological and mechanism genes, excluding zero-variance features; the resulting hepatocyte, endothelial and macrophage matrices contained 705, 717 and 716 genes. Supplementary Fig. S12A summarizes the localization and numerical-calibration sequence.

The original scTenifoldKnk analysis used scTenifoldpy 0.4.0, three seeds (20260904, 20260911 and 20260918), at most 60 sampled cells per donor, and 453–459 retained genes per run [36]. Complete network-construction, tensor-decomposition and alignment parameters are retained in Extended Methods and code. We audited raw unsigned network distances before interpreting percentile ranks. A maximum displacement ≤10−12 defined numerical zero, with sensitivity cut-offs of 10−14 and 10−10. All three thresholds yielded identical classification: 132/210 runs, including 73/102 drug-target runs, were numerical zero. Such outputs remained numerical diagnostics and were excluded from biological perturbation interpretation.

Networks used three resampled subnetworks, at most 250 sampled cells per subnetwork, three regression components and a 0.95 edge quantile. Rank-three PARAFAC decomposition used a 10−6 tolerance and 500-iteration limit; manifold alignment used 20 dimensions and a 10−8 tolerance. Single-gene and available canonical multi-target knockouts were retained under their actual gene compositions. Interrupted partial files were excluded. Original percentile, matched-null and seed-overlap summaries remained available for audit, but an apparently ordered percentile series did not override failure of the raw-distance criterion.

For each of the 29 nonzero drug-target runs, we weighted downstream clinical-protein displacement percentiles by normalized absolute confirmatory DO-versus-NDO Cliff's delta. Each protein was matched to 30 background genes in standardized log1p mean expression and logit detection, excluding clinical proteins and the knocked gene. Four thousand matched draws used the original cell-level reference, with replacement within pools; these matching features were not donor-balanced. The upper-tail P value was (1+null statistics at least as large as observed)/4,001. Alternative clinical weights and leave-one-protein-out analyses were diagnostic. No valid-run FDR discovery was declared, and numerical zero was not interpreted as biological inactivity. Unsigned displacement does not specify whether perturbation is protective or harmful.

2.7. Clinical-protein-weighted donor-balanced networks

Within each donor and compartment, genes were centered and standardized, with constant genes assigned zero vectors. Ledoit–Wolf covariance shrinkage was fitted before conversion to correlations and equal-donor averaging [39, 43]. The primary symmetric graph retained positive correlations and each gene's 20 strongest neighbors; an edge was retained if selected by either endpoint. Self-edges were removed, and isolated nodes received a self-loop only to define a stochastic transition. Sensitivity crossed positive versus absolute correlations, 10/20/40 neighbors and restart probabilities 0.3/0.5/0.7, yielding 18 graph specifications. An unshrunk Pearson ablation tested covariance regularization. Figure 5A summarizes the clinically weighted network integration.

Primary seed weights were normalized absolute discovery DO-versus-HV Cliff's deltas for all 13 proteins. Three additional profiles used discovery DO versus NDO, confirmatory DO versus HV, and confirmatory DO versus NDO. Random walk with restart propagated these continuous weights [41, 42]. For column-stochastic transition matrix P, seed vector v and restart probability r, the score was f=r[I−(1−r)P]−1v; primary r=0.5. Double-precision adjoint solutions required maximum absolute residual <10−10, stochasticity error <10−12 and nonnegative scores. Protein-specific contributions were retained and sum to the target score. Unsigned seed weights quantify proximity to an injury-associated pattern, not regulatory direction.

Null seeds excluded all clinical proteins and candidate targets. Each seed was matched to its 50 nearest background genes in standardized log(1+equal-donor mean expression) and logit detection with a symmetric 0.005 pseudocount. The 1,999 null sets sampled without duplicate genes within a set and inherited the clinical weights. Target nodes remained fixed. Empirical enrichment P values used a plus-one correction; Z was the observed-minus-null mean divided by null standard deviation, and zero null variance produced an undefined Z. BH correction jointly covered the 50 available target–compartment hypotheses within each setting. FGFR2 lacked an endothelial value, giving 50/51 possible combinations. Additional matching on log(1+weighted degree), with pools of 25/50/100 genes, tested topology sensitivity.

Each donor was omitted in turn, and graphs and matching features were rebuilt from the remaining donors while retaining the inherited feature universe. Rank stability required primary q<0.05, top-quartile rank in ≥75% of the 18 graph specifications and in ≥4/5 donor omissions. Expression-supported priority additionally required ≥10% equal-donor detection in the same compartment. These thresholds were not relaxed for sparse hits. All 72 graph-by-clinical-profile combinations were retained; correlated specifications were sensitivity analyses, not independent replications.

2.8. Integrated ranking and algorithm comparison

The clinical-network feature for target t was Nt=Σc(dtcZtc)/Σcdtc across available compartments, where detection d and null-adjusted proximity Z always came from the same compartment. This descriptive pooled feature was not subjected to a standard-normal Z test. Three archived features were unchanged: pharmacology combined maximum-pChEMBL percentile, median-pChEMBL percentile and source-confidence values with weights 0.55/0.25/0.20; HepG2 combined absolute dose-correlation and maximum-|z| percentiles with weights 0.60/0.40, multiplied by measurement reliability of 1.0/0.8/0.5 for landmark/best-inferred/other-inferred genes; STRING combined target-to-clinical-protein path-score and weighted-degree percentiles with weights 0.65/0.35 [55]. Exact definitions and the retained 227-edge STRING network are supplied in Extended Methods.

We reproduced the historical median-rank consensus, including mean-rank tiebreaking, and compared it with five rules using the four-feature matrix: median, arithmetic mean, geometric mean, maximin and a robust-rank-aggregation order-statistic score [40]. Feature values were converted to midpoint percentiles (rank−0.5)/number observed. Equal-weight geometric aggregation was fixed as primary before external assessment. Missing values received a conservative 0.5/17 percentile, with neutral and observed-feature-only sensitivities. A measured-landmark-only analysis masked all inferred HepG2 features simultaneously.

Twenty thousand competitive permutations shuffled clinical-network and STRING features jointly, with pharmacology and HepG2 shuffled separately, while preserving each target's missingness pattern. BH correction covered 17 targets within each integration algorithm. This calibration tests alignment under the stated rank-exchangeability model and does not estimate mediation probabilities. Component omission assessed ordering retention, top-five Jaccard overlap and agreement with the omitted feature; 4,999 label permutations supplied diagnostic agreement P values. Integrated rankings were recalculated across all 72 graph/clinical profiles and five donor omissions, with detection weights recalculated after omission. Five thousand Dirichlet(4,4,4,4) weight draws quantified rank intervals and top-five frequencies. These are robustness diagnostics rather than classification accuracy or biological CIs. Stochastic calculations used seed 20260919.

The robust-rank comparator used the minimum beta order-statistic tail probability of sorted lower-is-better percentile complements, with the probability clipped to [10−300, 1] before −log10 transformation. Its analytical independent-list probability was not used as a biological P value. Empirical permutation calibration was applied to this score in the same manner as to the other integration rules. Candidate output ranks used descending average ranks; top-five membership required rank ≤5. The comparison therefore separates exact reproduction of the historical workflow from alternative aggregation of identical four-feature inputs, while leaving the external liver measurements entirely outside score construction.

2.9. Single and joint target interventions in the clinical network

We applied perturbation-delta benchmarking and cell-context applicability principles from State [56] and explicit matrix and donor provenance from scBaseCount [57]. We retained the deposited normalized GSE115469 matrices and their five donor identifiers; no State checkpoint, scBaseCount-derived observations or new experimental perturbation data entered the analysis. Within the fixed hepatocyte, endothelial and macrophage gene universes, we reconstructed the original equal-donor Ledoit–Wolf positive top-20-neighbour graph with restart probability 0.5 and discovery DO-versus-HV clinical-protein weights. All incident edges were removed for PDGFRA, FLT4 or both targets, isolated nodes received self-loops, and the column-stochastic transition was rebuilt before propagation. The primary magnitude was the clinical-weighted absolute difference between intervened and baseline propagation scores divided by the weighted baseline score. The joint residual was the dual-intervention score minus both single-intervention scores plus the baseline. KDR and FGFR1 supplied comparator deletions; sham deletion and reapplication of the original graph checked numerical consistency. These operations change network proximity and do not estimate transcript abundance or biological rescue.

Each target was matched to 50 background genes on standardized log1p equal-donor expression, logit detection with a 0.005 pseudocount and log1p weighted degree, excluding the 13 clinical genes and 17 pharmacological candidates. All single-target controls and unique unordered matched pairs were evaluated: 1275, 1939 and 1275 pairs in hepatocyte, endothelial and macrophage networks, respectively. Upper-tail empirical probabilities used a plus-one correction. BH adjustment covered nine intervention-by-compartment magnitude tests and, separately, three joint-residual tests. Each donor was omitted in turn and the graph rebuilt within the inherited feature universe. A fixed 0%, 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% attenuation series scaled every incident edge once. Donor-omission ranges describe within-atlas sensitivity. Settings were fixed before calculation on 2 October 2026, after the original candidate ranking was known, and were not prospectively preregistered. Supplementary Fig. S11 and Additional files 4 and 5 retain all outcomes.

2.10. Independent hepatic pharmacodynamics

GSE125975 was withheld from candidate construction and algorithm selection. Its 22 distinct male C57BL/6J mice comprised three healthy controls, nine DEN/CCl4 disease controls and ten diseased animals receiving oral nintedanib 50 mg/kg; non-tumoral liver was collected at 17 weeks of age [58]. The source did not establish nintedanib-induced hepatotoxicity, and the dataset was not classified as DILI-positive. The independent evaluation design is shown in Fig. 6A.

We analysed all 13,392 features of the deposited log2 RMA matrix, using the custom HTMG430PM_Mm_ENTREZG Brainarray version 19 platform. We did not claim raw-CEL reprocessing. NCBI identifiers mapped 13,311 features to current symbols; 81 retained Entrez identifiers. The platform contains 17,856 probesets, but the available metadata do not identify the filter removing 4,464 features. Missing candidates were consequently unavailable, not zero. Shared uppercase ortholog symbols supported cross-species comparison; human CES1 was not silently mapped to one arbitrary mouse Ces1-family member.

A three-group model estimated treatment-versus-disease, disease-versus-healthy and treatment-versus-healthy contrasts. Residual variances used a Python empirical-Bayes scaled-F prior (residual degrees of freedom 19, estimated prior degrees of freedom 5.873 and prior variance 0.05022), not the R limma package. Moderated t tests and CIs used total degrees of freedom, with genome-wide BH correction by contrast; Welch tests supplied sensitivity to variance assumptions. For prespecified targets and proteins, exact two-sided tests enumerated all 92,378 allocations of ten treated among nineteen diseased animals. BH correction was reported for available members and conservatively for complete prespecified families, with unavailable values assigned P=1 only during correction. Seven standardized gene modules formed a separate multiplicity family.

Leave-one-animal estimates, 126 disjoint disease-control partitions and an inferred two-run-date adjustment tested sensitivity. Run dates came from CEL filenames rather than verified batch annotations; conditional randomization retained observed within-run treatment counts across 38,610 allocations. External algorithm comparison restricted all six fixed rankings to the same twelve measurable candidates and correlated priority with absolute treatment effects. Each whole-animal permutation recalculated all twelve effects together, preserving inter-gene dependence; BH correction covered six algorithms. This tests hepatic pharmacodynamic rank association, not clinical DILI prediction or genetic target rescue.

2.11. Pulmonary-response and tissue-context analyses

GSE278200 contained independent lung profiles from four rats per saline, bleomycin and bleomycin+nintedanib group; GSE308578 contained ten control, sixteen diseased-vehicle and seventeen nintedanib-treated mice [44, 45]. GEO/ARRIVE metadata resolved the latter source's interchanged figure-caption group counts. Gene-symbol counts underwent CPM filtering and TMM normalization. Differential expression used a transparent Python mean–variance-trend and empirical-Bayes implementation, with genome-wide BH adjustment, rather than the R limma package. Prespecified seven-module contrasts used animal-level tests and seven-module BH correction. No pulmonary dataset tuned the target ranking.

The rat regimen was oral nintedanib 100 mg/kg once daily from days 7–28 after bleomycin, with collection on day 28. The mouse regimen was 60 mg/kg twice daily from day 8 for 21 days, also collected on day 28. Filtering required CPM ≥1 in at least the smallest group size. TMM factors used 30% log-ratio and 5% absolute-expression trimming. A LOWESS trend with fraction 0.40 and two robustifying iterations modeled log residual variance, followed by 5th/95th-percentile Winsorization and moment-based scaled-F moderation. Module scores averaged available gene z scores within animal; coverage was reported rather than silently replacing missing members.

Because disease and treatment contrasts share disease controls, we repeated reversal analysis using deposited Ensembl count features, a label-independent CPM filter and log2-CPM normalization. An effect-only disease set required |log2 change|≥0.5. Reversal fraction, disease–treatment correlation and median |d|−|d+t| were recalculated after every whole-animal allocation: all 70 allocations in rats and 1,000 distinct allocations in mice. Directional P values and BH correction covered six dataset–statistic tests. All six oriented rat disease-reference splits and 500 eight-versus-eight mouse splits provided disjoint-reference sensitivities; their ranges are descriptive and not independent-replication CIs.

Additional sources retained their actual experimental units. GSE120804 liver slices and GSE120679 lung slices originated from pooled rat tissue and supplied descriptive slice-level context [59, 60]. PXD024058 had conflicting biological/technical replicate descriptions and was analysed without inferential P values [61]. GSE151374 comprised 18 independently prepared single-cell libraries pooling three mice each; pool-level pseudobulk and exact 3-versus-3 tests, not cells, supplied inference [46]. PXD052594 included all 23 deposited lung proteomes (10 treated, 13 vehicle), without post-treatment radiomic selection; postrandomization attrition was retained as a limitation [47]. Its 7,006-protein and 20,043-phosphosite families were analysed separately. GSE299128 supplied 57 longitudinal blood samples from seven participants without adjudicated DILI; participant-specific slopes used all 128 sign assignments and 30-gene BH correction. Full eligibility, preprocessing, pooling, attrition and multiplicity details remain in Extended Methods.

2.12. Reproducibility and statistical units

Sample sizes were determined by available source data. Tests were two-sided unless the competitive or restoration alternative was explicitly directional. Effect sizes, CIs, observed denominators and unavailable measurements were retained alongside BH-adjusted results. No discordant dataset was removed to improve concordance. Donors, animals, pooled libraries, participants or safety reports supplied the applicable units; cells, features and repeated computational seeds did not increase biological sample size. The analysis code and associated documentation record source-specific environments, software versions, random seeds, parameters and SHA-256 checksums. Frozen-output verification and regeneration from newly downloaded repository data are distinguished where complete upstream raw datasets require retrieval from the original sources.

OpenAI ChatGPT with Codex tools supported literature retrieval, analytical code development and checking, visualization and manuscript preparation. Source-linked data, numerical consistency checks and author review governed the use of generated outputs. Inductive Bio model versions and inputs are specified above; further details are provided in the Acknowledgments.

No new human samples, cell experiments or animal experiments were generated. The supplementary perturbation-and-rescue protocol is prospective and contributes no observed result.

3. Results

3.1. Hepatic adverse-event reporting motivates molecular target prioritization

The openFDA release contained 30233 mutually exclusive nintedanib-indexed and 39467 pirfenidone-indexed reports across all recorded indications. Narrow hepatic events occurred in 340 and 112 reports, respectively, giving a reporting odds ratio (ROR) of 3.997 (95% confidence interval [CI] 3.226–4.951). The broad endpoint occurred in 1773 and 717 reports (ROR 3.367; 95% CI 3.083–3.677). Expanded product aliases gave similar estimates. An exploratory aminotransferase-plus-bilirubin/jaundice constellation involved only 19 versus 12 reports and was not classified as Hy's law (Fig. 1B,C).

Restriction to a reported interstitial-lung-disease indication retained the association: narrow ROR 3.682 (95% CI 2.911–4.658) and broad ROR 3.928 (95% CI 3.565–4.329). Filters requiring any suspect drug and their intersection with the indication filter were also directionally consistent (Fig. 1D). These were report-level sensitivities: openFDA array flattening can assign the indication or suspect role to a co-reported drug. The same-element case audit clarified this linkage without supplying a corresponding noncase denominator (Supplementary Fig. S1). Sex and age strata were compatible with the overall signal but did not establish demographic interactions (Fig. 1H,I).

Calendar time materially affected magnitude. Tests rejected homogeneous annual odds ratios for both endpoints. The 2022 narrow-endpoint contribution comprised 123 nintedanib reports and none for pirfenidone; 104/123 originated in Italy and 96/123 were received in July. Excluding 2022 reduced the narrow ROR to 2.550 (95% CI 2.029–3.205), while the 2023–2026 estimate was 1.464 (95% CI 0.928–2.309; Fig. 1E–G). Among reports with eligible exact dates, median narrow-endpoint onset was 15 days for nintedanib and 65 days for pirfenidone, with substantial missingness. Broad-endpoint onset distributions and overlapping serious outcomes were descriptive (Fig. 1J,K). These findings motivate receptor-level investigation of the hepatic safety signal, with the calendar analyses defining its temporal context.

3.2. Human serum proteins specify the injury phenotype for target screening

We next defined what a candidate mechanism should explain. The human serum workbook provided observations for 13 source-selected proteins, but lacked identifiers supporting a defensible participant-by-protein matrix. We therefore retained protein-specific denominators and analyzed the measured distributions without constructing a multivariable predictor.

FBP1 was the only candidate separating DILI onset from acute non-drug liver injury after correction across the 13-protein family. Values were lower in DILI onset (median 0.00770; 76/82 detected) than in non-drug injury (median 0.02075; 32/34 detected), with Cliff's delta −0.505 (95% CI −0.724 to −0.288; q=4.69×10−4). With higher non-drug-injury values defined as positive, its apparent univariate area under the curve was 0.753 (95% CI 0.644–0.862; Fig. 2D,E,I). Half-minimum substitution for non-detection retained the contrast (delta −0.455; q=0.00156; Fig. 2J). Relative to healthy volunteers, however, FBP1 was higher in DILI (delta 0.922; 95% CI 0.847–0.978; q=4.02×10−15). Its meaning is therefore contextual: it distinguishes injury from health and, within the released panel, helps separate two acute-injury etiologies. This source-reported FBP1 contrast [30] provides an etiologically informative readout within the clinical phenotype used to guide receptor screening.

The broader panel supplied the phenotype used for network evaluation. Eleven proteins showed at least moderate, false-discovery-rate (FDR)-controlled separation between DILI onset and health; ACO1, ASS1, and FAH had the largest effects (Cliff's deltas 0.978, 0.967, and 0.954; Fig. 2A–C). Among 12 evaluable, non-duplicated discovery candidates, 11 retained the DILI-versus-non-DILI direction in confirmation, with descriptive effect-rank correlation 0.561 (Fig. 2F). These correlated proteins were not treated as independent replication units. Nine of 11 proteins with available follow-up blocks were lower than at onset, but absent participant identifiers prevented paired recovery analysis (Fig. 2H).

Within DILI, ALT correlated strongly with ASS1 (ρ=0.873), ACO1 (0.800), and ALDOB (0.798), and also with the complementary readouts OTC (0.701), GSTA1 (0.668), and FBP1 (0.425; Fig. 2G). No liver-zone comparison survived FDR correction. Protein-specific distributions, detection patterns, source-row discrepancies, censoring, and duplicate-series audits are preserved in Supplementary Figs. S2 and S3. We carried all 13 proteins forward as continuous phenotype measurements; FBP1 provided the strongest etiologic contrast, while most other proteins primarily reflected injury or severity. This combination links etiologic discrimination with complementary measures of injury burden in the downstream experimental phenotype.

3.3. Drug pharmacology and cellular responses define the inputs for liver-context integration

To connect this serum phenotype to a perturbation, we examined nintedanib-exposed HepG2 cells and curated drug-binding evidence. Six 24-h consensus dose profiles covered 12328 genes. No monotonic gene response passed genome-wide FDR correction (minimum q=0.268); the exact six-dose test had a minimum attainable two-sided P value of 0.00278 (Fig. 3A–C). These exposure-response profiles supplied quantitative cellular features for the subsequent integrated ranking.

Twelve clinical-protein genes were represented. Their measurement-stratum-adjusted response percentile was 0.497 against a matched-null mean of 0.500 (competitive P=0.511), giving no enrichment of the injury set (Fig. 3D,E). OTC and LECT2 each had dose correlation 0.943, and GSTA1 had correlation 0.886, but none passed anchor-family correction. FBP1 was nonmonotonic across the six doses (ρ=0.029; exact P=1.000). No prespecified mechanism module survived FDR correction (Fig. 3F). Overlap with truncated MCF7 chemical-transcriptomic lists was positive but small: correlations were 0.086 near 1 µM and 0.140 at 10 µM. FBP1 appeared with a positive sign in both MCF7 lists, a narrowly defined cross-model observation rather than biological-replicate validation (Fig. 3G,H).

ChEMBL supplied 2250 activity records, of which 300 exact-relation, validity-clean human single-protein activities mapped to 222 targets. Sixteen targets had potent support in at least two documents. Canonical maximum pChEMBL values included KDR 8.98, PDGFRA 8.72, FLT4 8.30, FGFR3 8.23, FGFR1 7.50, and FGFR2 7.43; none of the 13 serum proteins had qualifying direct-target evidence in the curated ChEMBL records (Fig. 3I). Among the 16 represented multi-document targets, potency did not correlate with HepG2 response magnitude (ρ=−0.074; P=0.787; Fig. 3J). A secondary signed-response analysis favored a positive canonical-target-set shift, but its magnitude test was null. Measurement-stratified, dose-omission, and cross-cell-line sensitivities are shown in Supplementary Fig. S4. The combined result defines distinct quantities to connect experimentally: biochemical target engagement, intracellular transcriptional response, and extracellular injury-protein release.

3.4. Experimental biochemical records support engagement of both prioritized receptors

The primary pharmacology set contained 11 quantitative records for the two priorities: seven for PDGFRA and four for FLT4. Direct binding measurements reported Kd values of 16 nM for PDGFRA and 95 nM for FLT4 in the same kinase-binding study [54], complementing the cellular-context ranking with measured receptor-binding evidence (Fig. 4A,B). PDGFRA IC50 values spanned 1.9–560 nM across five source documents, with a median of 18 nM; FLT4 IC50 values spanned 5–34 nM across three documents, with a median of 13 nM. A separate PDGFRA Ki was 8.4 nM. Thus, both receptors had submicromolar experimental pharmacological support, while direct affinity and inhibitory potency remained distinct endpoints.

Within the two primary documents reporting both receptors, PDGFRA-to-FLT4 IC50 ratios were 3.60 and 4.54. Three additional records entered the labelled assay-format sensitivity; the expanded IC50 medians were 11 nM for PDGFRA and 5 nM for FLT4, and two additional matched-document ratios were 0.63 and 0.72 (Fig. 4C,D). The variable relative ordering and document-omission profiles support engagement of both receptors without establishing uniform selectivity for either receptor (Fig. 4E,F). These endpoint-resolved findings elaborate the original pharmacology source rather than add an independent validation cohort. Inductive Bio returned LogD7.4 2.523, acidic pKa 10.945 and basic pKa 7.712, with no low-confidence or out-of-domain flags; the full returned bounds and configurations are reported in Additional files 4 and 5.

3.5. Clinical-protein-guided integration prioritizes PDGFRA and FLT4

Integration of the clinical-network feature with pharmacology, HepG2 response and STRING association context prioritized PDGFRA and FLT4 (Fig. 5L). The primary geometric ordering was PDGFRA, FLT4, FGFR2, JAK1, and FGFR3; FGFR1 moved from third in the historical consensus to fourteenth (Fig. 5F,G). This ranking followed the same fixed candidate set and a rule selected before the independent liver challenge. PDGFRA and FLT4 occupied the first two positions under median, arithmetic, geometric, and maximin fusion; robust rank aggregation placed them first and third. Both remained in the top five across all 5000 source-weight perturbations, compared with 92.9% for FGFR2, 70.0% for JAK1, and 58.9% for FGFR3 (Fig. 5J). Measurable endothelial FLT4 expression identifies a cellular starting point for perturbation, while PDGFRA prioritization directs receptor-expression verification in an appropriately sampled hepatic system.

Across 72 fixed graph-and-clinical-weight combinations, geometric rank correlations ranged from 0.782 to 1.000 (median 0.946), with mean top-five overlap 0.949. PDGFRA remained first after each donor omission; FLT4 ranked second to fifth (Fig. 5K). Alternative missing-value treatments preserved the leading five, while masking inferred HepG2 transcripts changed the overall ranking more substantially (Supplementary Fig. S8). Together, these analyses place PDGFRA and FLT4 at the front of a reproducible experimental sequence: verify receptor expression and engagement in the relevant hepatic compartment, measure its effects on the clinical injury-protein phenotype, and test reciprocal rescue.

Liver-cell localization placed FLT4 in the endothelial compartment and PDGFRA predominantly in the sparsely sampled stellate compartment, while the clinical injury proteins were mainly hepatocellular (Supplementary Fig. S12B–G). Numerical calibration excluded 132 of 210 virtual-knockout runs with noise-scale displacement; the remaining drug-target runs showed no enrichment of the clinical protein phenotype (minimum P=0.352; Supplementary Fig. S12H–K). These findings guided integration of clinical-network proximity with biochemical pharmacology and cellular drug responses.

The donor-balanced analysis evaluated 50 target–compartment combinations across the fixed 17-target universe (Fig. 5B). Primary computations passed numerical checks, and alternative graph, clinical-weight and donor settings supplied complementary tests of stability. Three proximity associations passed multiplicity correction, but no candidate jointly met significance, rank stability and the 10% same-compartment detection threshold (Fig. 5C,M). The macrophage PDGFRA association was dominated by HPD and arose from only two detected cells; seed decomposition, degree-matched calibration and donor omission therefore informed the choice of cellular systems (Fig. 5D,E). Detailed effect estimates and covariance sensitivities are reported in Supplementary Results.

Geometric fusion retained the ranking after component omission more consistently than the historical consensus (mean Spearman correlation 0.833 versus 0.793; mean top-five Jaccard overlap 0.607 versus 0.506; Fig. 5H). Agreement with the omitted feature remained weak, and none of the five integration methods reached competitive q<0.05 (Fig. 5I; Supplementary Results). Candidate stability therefore supports the order of experimental testing without establishing predictive accuracy.

Single and joint PDGFRA/FLT4 network interventions did not exceed matched-control backgrounds (all nine primary q≥0.529), and joint residuals were nonsignificant. These analyses define within-atlas sensitivity rather than a directional or synergistic receptor relationship (Supplementary Fig. S11; Supplementary Results).

3.6. Independent liver exposure identifies KDR as a pharmacodynamic comparator

We challenged the fixed priorities in GSE125975, an independent experiment containing 22 mouse non-tumor liver profiles: three healthy controls, nine disease controls, and ten nintedanib-treated animals [58] (Fig. 6B). The deposited matrix contained 13392 features. Nintedanib altered 92 at genome-wide q<0.05, including 73 with absolute log2 difference at least 0.5; Welch sensitivity analysis retained 13 discoveries. Disease versus healthy liver yielded 338 moderated-test discoveries, while treated versus healthy liver yielded 784, indicating an observable response without simple restoration to the healthy transcriptome (Fig. 6C,J).

Only 12 of 17 targets and two of 13 clinical-protein genes were represented. Kdr decreased with nintedanib (log2 difference −0.2796; moderated 95% CI −0.4489 to −0.1102). Its genome-wide q value was 0.1192, but exact whole-animal testing gave P=0.002273, q=0.0273 across the 12 measured candidates, and conservative q=0.0386 across the full 17-candidate family (Fig. 6D,E). The direction persisted after every treated- or disease-animal omission (range −0.3180 to −0.2425; Fig. 6H). Adjustment for two run-date groups inferred from CEL filenames retained the decrease and candidate-family significance (log2 difference −0.2768; genome-wide q=0.1114; conditional-randomization full-family q=0.02950). These date tokens were a technical sensitivity rather than verified batch annotations.

The Kdr response was concordant with reduced VEGFR2 phosphorylation and downstream AKT/ERK signaling reported in the same source experiment [58]. Together, the transcript and biochemical measurements from this source support use of the VEGFR arm as a hepatic pharmacodynamic comparator. The individual-animal profiles illustrate receptor-specific responses (Fig. 6E–G): Fgfr1 was essentially unchanged (log2 difference 0.0242; genome-wide q=0.9589), and no prespecified module passed exact-permutation correction (Fig. 6I). Of 297 disease-associated features meeting both statistical and effect thresholds, 51.2% changed oppositely under treatment; disjoint disease-reference analysis reduced median reversal to 46.0%, with median disease–treatment correlation 0.149 (Fig. 6K,L).

Finally, we tested whether priority predicted absolute hepatic expression-effect magnitude among the same 12 measured candidates. Correlations ranged from −0.274 for maximin to 0.252 for robust rank aggregation; geometric fusion yielded −0.252 (exact whole-animal P=0.461). All six method-level q values were at least 0.681 (Fig. 6M). The independent challenge identifies selective hepatic drug responsiveness and supports KDR as an organ-level pharmacodynamic comparator for subsequent testing of the PDGFRA and FLT4 priorities.

3.7. Pulmonary responses define the therapeutic constraint on a liver-sparing intervention

The same intervention must preserve nintedanib's pulmonary benefit. In GSE278200, four independent rats per group supplied a responsive fibrosis context: 895 genes changed with nintedanib at genome-wide q<0.05, including 551 with absolute log2 fold change at least 0.5. Among 3647 disease-responsive genes, 94.4% changed in the opposite direction under treatment in the original shared-reference analysis. Direct contrasts showed lower COL1A1 (log2 fold change −1.607; 95% CI −2.508 to −0.705; q=0.0404) and POSTN (−0.467; 95% CI −0.660 to −0.275; q=0.0188). No prespecified treatment module survived correction (Supplementary Fig. S9A–E).

The GSE308578 mouse model showed weaker response. Its deposited groups comprised 10 controls, 16 bleomycin–vehicle animals, and 17 nintedanib-treated animals, using concordant GEO and ARRIVE mapping rather than the swapped counts in the source figure caption. Although 1059 genes changed at q<0.05, only 208 met the effect-size threshold. The original shared-reference reversal fraction was 78.9%, with a smaller median reversal index than in rats (0.257 versus 0.514). ABCB1B increased by 0.519 log2 units (95% CI 0.271–0.767; q=0.00788), and its effect remained positive in all 33 animal omissions. No treatment module passed FDR correction (Supplementary Fig. S9F–H). This supports an efflux-associated, model-dependent boundary on response rather than universal inactivity of nintedanib.

We then calibrated global reversal against its shared-comparator structure, retaining all original gene-level results. Reanalysis of deposited count features gave shared-reference reversal fractions of 92.3% in rats and 78.4% in mice, with disease–treatment correlations of −0.827 and −0.625. Whole-animal permutations showed negative correlations and substantial reversal even under the null. No statistic survived correction across the six dataset–summary tests; restoration had q=0.1286 in both species (Supplementary Fig. S10). Disjoint disease-reference splits retained average directional reversal but reduced it to medians of 84.7% in rats and 73.9% in mice. Mouse correlation ranged from −0.737 to 0.185, and the contrast-based restoration proxy crossed zero. These overlapping-subset ranges describe sensitivity, not confidence intervals or independent replication. The direct COL1A1 and POSTN contrasts therefore provide clearer molecular evidence than an uncalibrated claim of global transcriptome restoration.

The hepatic and pulmonary context datasets further tested whether the proposed clinical-protein program behaved uniformly. In pooled bile-duct-ligation liver slices, nintedanib coincided with higher FBP1, OTC, and CPS1 transcripts and lower fibrosis-related transcripts (Supplementary Fig. S9I). These were slice-level observations from pooled animals, not replicated evidence of hepatoprotection. Pooled lung slices and the PXD024058 lung-protein matrix also showed heterogeneous target–protein directions (Supplementary Fig. S9J). Uncertain biological versus technical replication in PXD024058 precluded inferential P values.

Cellular and protein measurements further delimited pulmonary target confirmation. GSE151374 showed no FDR-controlled effects on the examined macrophage genes, composition or programs at the pooled-library level (Supplementary Fig. S6). In PXD052594, no protein passed q<0.10, and none of the quantified phosphosites in the examined receptor and downstream signaling tracks passed q<0.05 (Supplementary Fig. S7). Pooling, postrandomization losses and the absence of matched total-protein adjustment constrained these comparisons; complete sample counts, nominal effects and phosphoproteome results are retained in Supplementary Results.

Finally, 57 whole-blood samples from seven nintedanib-treated participants supplied no longitudinal target–protein confirmation: all 30 prespecified slope tests had q=1.00 (Supplementary Fig. S5). This single-arm series lacked adjudicated DILI and was not a direct test of hepatic injury. Taken together, these tissue and clinical sensitivities retain the drug's pulmonary purpose within the same mechanistic question. They support testing PDGFRA and FLT4 against receptor activity, intercellular signaling, and hepatocyte injury at matched exposure, followed by a direct demonstration that any liver-sparing effect preserves pulmonary antifibrotic activity.

4. Discussion

This study prioritizes PDGFRA and FLT4 for mechanistic investigation of nintedanib-associated liver injury and defines how to test them against a human injury-protein phenotype. Their leading positions persisted across alternative integration rules, source-weight perturbations, and donor omission, providing a focused basis for allocating experimental effort. The contribution is the connection between clinical-protein weighting, drug pharmacology, liver-cell context, and calibrated network integration within one translational sequence. The resulting framework advances a specific experimental question: how does perturbing PDGFRA or FLT4 alter hepatocyte injury at comparable drug exposure, and can that response be modified while maintaining pulmonary antifibrotic activity?

The hepatic reporting signal establishes the clinical relevance of this question and the importance of evaluating it across treatment contexts and calendar periods. Our full-period RORs agree with the safety concern described by earlier comparative studies [21, 22, 23, 24, 25]. The concentration of narrow-endpoint reports in 2022 and attenuation in the recent period show that report composition materially shapes its magnitude. Presenting all endpoint, indication, and calendar specifications follows pharmacovigilance reporting guidance and the transparency rationale of specification-curve analysis [49, 50, 51]. This evidence defines the clinical safety problem that the protein-guided receptor analysis is designed to investigate.

The clinical proteome gives that question a measurable endpoint. Changes in intermediary-metabolism, nitrogen-handling, and detoxification proteins provide a more specific experimental phenotype than a generic injury gene list. This interpretation fits the development of mechanistic biomarkers and the need to qualify them for a particular clinical use [31, 62, 63]. FBP1 is especially informative because its direction depends on the comparator: higher in DILI than in health, but lower than in acute non-drug injury. Our reanalysis extends the interpretation of this source-reported candidate [30] by using its etiologic contrast to guide receptor screening within a broader human injury-protein phenotype. Measuring intracellular FBP1 activity together with extracellular release and viability is necessary to distinguish metabolic regulation from cell damage, altered clearance, or tissue loss.

The relationship between receptor activity, transcription, and protein release determines the measurements needed to test these candidates. Kinase inhibition can change phosphorylation without changing receptor transcript abundance; extracellular injury-protein concentrations can rise without increased intracellular transcription. Accordingly, the assays should resolve the sequence linking receptor engagement, cellular response, and injury-protein release. LINCS and chemical single-cell transcriptomics provide exposure-response information [34, 35] that can guide the design of primary multicellular hepatic experiments. The cellular separation between endothelial receptor expression and hepatocellular injury proteins gives a plausible setting in which to investigate such interactions. Liver atlases and fibrotic-niche studies support the relevance of this organization [32, 64] and motivate compartment-resolved testing of candidate intercellular mediators.

Numerical calibration established which virtual-knockout outputs could enter biological interpretation. Rank transformation can order numerical noise; retaining raw distances and applying scale checks prevented those outputs from determining receptor priority. The calibrated network framework then linked candidate selection to the clinical phenotype on an interpretable scale.

The donor-balanced network analysis connects receptor selection to a clinically weighted injury phenotype within a donor-balanced liver reference. Covariance shrinkage, random walk with restart, and rank aggregation are established methods [39, 40, 41, 42, 43]; their value here arises from combining them with continuous human protein effects, numerical and expression checks, actual donor-omission reconstruction, and matched-background calibration. These steps address different sources of instability before experimental resources are committed. Seed decomposition and compartment-specific detection further indicate where an apparent connection needs targeted biological sampling. This clinically specified and auditable integration complements the broader effort to benchmark inferred networks and perturbation responses against appropriate baselines [36, 37, 38, 65].

Algorithm comparison showed that the leading receptor priorities persisted across specified changes in aggregation, source weighting and donor composition. The weak omitted-feature and independent hepatic rank associations distinguish this reproducibility from predictive validation. Together, these analyses support a focused experimental order whose biological value can be tested directly.

The endpoint-resolved pharmacology adds a direct biochemical basis for this experimental choice: both receptors have measured nanomolar binding affinity, alongside inhibitory activity across multiple source documents. Assay-dependent relative potency argues for measuring occupancy or proximal receptor phosphorylation at the same intracellular nintedanib exposure in the selected hepatic systems. The network interventions assess sensitivity to receptor-node deletion; their nonsignificant matched-background effects leave the biological direction and interaction between PDGFRA and FLT4 unresolved. State emphasizes evaluation against held-out measured perturbations within the model’s supported intervention domain [56], while scBaseCount provides principles for improving reference-cell provenance and coverage [57]. Together, these considerations focus the next validation on target-expressing hepatic contexts and experimentally measured perturbation responses.

PDGFRA and FLT4 provide complementary entry points into the hypothesis that non-parenchymal receptor signaling influences hepatocyte susceptibility during nintedanib exposure. PDGFRA combines strong biochemical support with stable integrated priority; its stellate-associated localization directs initial testing toward adequately sampled stellate-cell–hepatocyte systems. Receptor protein and activity should first be measured in those systems, because the macrophage proximity signal was dominated by HPD and arose from only two detected cells. FLT4 has clearer endothelial detection, making sinusoidal endothelial–hepatocyte co-culture a defined starting point. For each receptor, time-resolved perturbation, conditioned-medium transfer, direct co-culture, and reciprocal rescue can test the sequence from receptor activity through candidate secreted mediators to the hepatocyte injury phenotype. Comparing receptor inhibition with restoration will test whether either direction improves tolerance at matched exposure.

Independent liver exposure adds KDR as a pharmacodynamic comparator to this experimental strategy. The candidate-family Kdr decrease and reduced VEGFR2 phosphorylation reported in the same source experiment indicate hepatic responsiveness of nintedanib's VEGFR arm [58]. Measuring this comparator alongside PDGFRA and FLT4 can help separate effective hepatic drug engagement from changes in injury-protein release. Angiocrine control of liver regeneration and recent endothelial FGF1–hepatocyte FGFR4 work provide examples of intercellular metabolic regulation that inform assay design [66, 67]. Receptor activity, mediator release, and hepatocyte metabolic function should therefore be resolved over time in the same exposure-controlled system.

FGFR1 supplies an additional comparator for compartment-specific testing. Its context-dependent biology includes vascular-niche fibrosis, hepatocyte detoxification during regeneration, and macrophage inflammatory regulation [68, 69, 70]. Testing it alongside PDGFRA and FLT4 would directly examine whether the integrated experimental priorities better capture the relevant injury response. Increased metabolic transcripts in nintedanib-exposed liver slices and reported antifibrotic effects in experimental liver disease further indicate that tissue state should be incorporated into the design [59, 71, 72]. Parallel uninjured and disease-conditioned hepatic systems, with intracellular drug quantification, can distinguish altered drug disposition from improved cellular tolerance at comparable exposure.

Pulmonary preservation completes this experimental logic. The responsive rat contrasts and the weaker mouse response show why a liver-sparing intervention should first be assessed in a pulmonary system with demonstrated drug activity and then challenged in another model [44, 45]. Animal-level permutation and disjoint-reference analyses also show why a large negative disease–treatment correlation is insufficient evidence of restoration. Cell and protein datasets add tests of generalization while retaining their pool or animal denominators [46, 47]. The decisive outcome is preserved suppression of fibroblast activation, matrix production, and functional or histological fibrosis at an exposure that reduces hepatic injury. Efforts to separate pulmonary delivery from systemic exposure emphasize the clinical value of this objective [73, 74].

The next study should implement donor-replicated hepatic perturbation of PDGFRA and FLT4, with KDR and FGFR1 comparators, and measure receptor activity, candidate secreted mediators, intracellular metabolism, and extracellular FBP1, GSTA1, and OTC together. Reciprocal rescue should follow an exposure-controlled injury effect. Parent nintedanib, BIBF 1202, and relevant downstream metabolites should be quantified to distinguish disposition from cellular tolerance [28, 29]. Prospective clinical sampling can then connect serial injury proteins and pharmacokinetics to adjudicated liver-test changes and pulmonary response. Existing circulating-biomarker studies support the feasibility of serial assessment [75, 76]. New spatial transcriptomic and protein atlases can refine localization and guide selection of the definitive cellular system [77, 78].

Several limitations define the scope of these findings. The prioritization establishes a reproducible experimental order, rather than a causal DILI target or a protective intervention direction: competitive calibration did not confirm a candidate, and the fixed rankings were not significantly associated with hepatic transcript-response magnitude. No public cohort jointly measured nintedanib exposure, adjudicated DILI, target perturbation, and the serum protein panel. The proteomic candidates were selected by the source study, and missing identifiers prevented paired or multivariable analyses. The liver networks use five healthy donors and a fixed, previously selected gene universe; donor omission does not repeat that upstream feature selection. PDGFRA localization requires improved stellate-cell sampling, and the unsigned network scores require bidirectional experimental perturbation. The six HepG2 consensus doses do not estimate biological variation at each concentration, and inferred transcripts influence ranking. Analysis settings were fixed before the independent liver challenge but were not prospectively preregistered. Cross-species exposure, tissue state, pooling, postrandomization losses, and incomplete measurements constrain the preclinical comparisons. Donor-replicated multicellular hepatic models, exposure-controlled perturbation and rescue, and prospective clinical protein sampling provide a direct route to address these gaps.

5. Conclusions

Clinical-protein-guided integration prioritizes PDGFRA and FLT4 for experimental investigation of nintedanib-associated liver injury. Their stability across aggregation rules, source-weight perturbations, and donor omission focuses the next stage of research on two receptor candidates with distinct hepatic cellular contexts. The framework connects drug pharmacology to a measurable human injury phenotype and supplies KDR as a hepatic pharmacodynamic comparator. The translational objective is to establish the receptor-to-injury sequence, identify a rescuable hepatocellular response at matched drug exposure, and preserve pulmonary antifibrotic activity.
