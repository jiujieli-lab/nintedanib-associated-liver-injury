28 Study design and unified target-to-injury analysis

29 We investigated whether nintedanib pharmacology can be connected to a clinically measured

30 hepatocellular injury phenotype through liver-cell networks, and whether the resulting

31 experimental priorities withstand numerical calibration, alternative integration algorithms, and

32 biological-sample sensitivity. We followed observational-study reporting principles where

33 applicable [48] and READUS-PV recommendations for pharmacovigilance [49, 50]. The

34 analysis began with an active-comparator hepatic-event reporting signal and the human DILI

35 protein phenotype. It then tested drug response and cellular localization, incorporated the

36 continuous protein effects into perturbation and network calculations, and evaluated the resulting

37 priorities against experimental tissue responses. Pulmonary analyses addressed the antifibrotic

38 response that a liver-sparing intervention would need to preserve.

39 The pharmacologic candidate universe contained 17 proteins and the clinical phenotype

40 contained the 13 proteins available from the human DILI source study. All candidates were

41 retained through the revised comparison. Drug binding, circulating protein abundance, transcript

42 response, and network proximity were connected as distinct measurements of the same proposed

43 target-to-injury process; one measurement was not relabelled as another. Correlated features

44 from the same data source did not create independent biological replications. The revised




                                                      2
45 analysis used continuous clinical effects rather than positive and negative target labels, and did

46 not estimate individual DILI risk from unlinked public observations.

47 The initial source-specific analyses and parameters were frozen in the original analysis

48 manifests. Additional numerical auditing, clinical-weighted projection, donor-balanced shrinkage

49 networks, rank-aggregation comparisons, and shared-reference sensitivity analyses were

50 developed on 19 September 2026. Their parameter grids and final integration

51 rule were fixed before inspection of the newly identified GSE125975 liver results. These

52 additional analyses are not presented as prospectively preregistered. Dataset inclusion

53 was based on exposure, tissue, comparison, data availability, and experimental-unit provenance;

54 disagreement with a preferred candidate was not an exclusion criterion. The complete

55 enumerated sensitivities were retained rather than selecting the parameter setting yielding the

56 most favorable molecule.

 97 Disproportionality estimands and uncertainty

 98 For a 2×2 report table with nintedanib endpoint and non-endpoint counts a and b and pirfenidone

 99 counts c and d, the reporting odds ratio was ROR = (a × d)/(b × c), and the proportional reporting

100 ratio was PRR = [a/(a+b)]/[c/(c+d)]. Wald 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated on the

101 log scale; the ROR standard error was sqrt(1/a + 1/b + 1/c + 1/d), and the PRR standard error

102 was sqrt[1/a−1/(a+b)+1/c−1/(c+d)]. A Haldane–Anscombe correction of 0.5 was applied only

103 when at least one 2×2 cell was zero. When both arms had zero endpoint reports, the contrast was

104 non-estimable rather than continuity-corrected. We additionally reported two-sided Fisher exact

105 tests and Pearson chi-square tests with Yates correction.

106 We implemented an active-comparator Bayesian information component as IC =

107 log2[p11/{(p11+p10)(p11+p01)}]. Its posterior median and 95% credible interval were obtained

108 from 100,000 draws from a Jeffreys–Dirichlet posterior with 0.5 added to each cell. This

109 transparent active-comparator estimator was not described as the WHO production BCPNN

110 algorithm.

111 Calendar-year-specific estimates used the same 2×2 definition. We calculated a Mantel–

112 Haenszel ROR with Robins–Breslow–Greenland 95% CI and a 30,000-replicate within-year

113 multinomial parametric-bootstrap sensitivity interval, with 0.5 stabilization inside bootstrap

115 adjusted Breslow–Day test, both across the full window and after excluding 2022. We also

116 summarized inverse-variance heterogeneity across estimable annual log-RORs with Cochran’s Q

117 and I²=max[0,(Q−df)/Q]×100%. Because the homogeneity tests were strongly positive, Mantel–

220 HepG2 LINCS L1000 concentration-response analysis

221 We extracted nintedanib perturbagen BRD-K49075727 from the official GSE70138 signature

222 metadata generated by the LINCS L1000 framework [34] by exact perturbagen, HepG2 cell line,

223 and 24-h exposure. Six Level 5 composite signatures were available at 0.04, 0.12, 0.37, 1.11,

224 3.33, and 10 µM; their deposited constituent-instance counts were 3, 3, 3, 2, 3, and 3. The matrix

225 contained 12,328 genes: 978 directly measured landmark genes and 11,350 inferred genes, with

226 inferred transcripts further divided into best-inferred (BING) and other-inferred strata. Level 5

227 signatures are replicate-collapsed consensus z scores; the six doses were therefore not treated as

228 six independent biological cohorts.

229 For each gene, we related Level 5 z score to log10 concentration using Spearman’s rank

230 correlation. With six dose summaries, we calculated exact two-sided P values by enumerating all

231 6! = 720 dose-label permutations. BH correction was applied across all 12,328 genes; we also

232 calculated within-stratum q values for measured landmarks, BING, and other inferred genes. We

233 summarized amplitude as maximum absolute z score, integrated response as trapezoidal area

234 under the z-score curve over log10 dose divided by the observed log-dose range, and direction

235 consistency as the fraction of doses sharing the sign of the normalized area. The non-inferential

236 prioritization statistic was |Spearman ρ| × maximum |z|. Leave-one-dose-out analysis recorded

237 the median correlation, the minimum absolute correlation, and the proportion of five-dose

238 estimates retaining the full-series direction.

317 Numerical calibration and clinically weighted virtual perturbation

318 Before using the previous virtual-knockout results as a comparator, we audited the raw distances

319 for every target, compartment, and random-seed combination. A run with maximum




                                                     14
320 displacement at or below 10−12 was designated numerically zero. This threshold was evaluated at

321 10−14 and 10−10 as well. The audit identified 132 of 210 runs with maximum distances at or below

322 2.85 × 10−15, including 73 of 102 drug-target runs; the smallest maximum distance in the nonzero

323 group was 4.73 × 10−7. Thus, the three tested thresholds yielded identical classification and

324 retained 29 drug-target runs for quantitative clinical projection. Percentile normalization can

325 assign a complete 0–1 ordering even when all underlying values are floating-point-scale

326 differences; such orderings cannot support biological perturbation inference. We therefore

327 retained these runs for the numerical benchmark but excluded them from the analysis of

376 Donor-aware covariance estimation and graph construction

377 For each compartment and donor separately, we centered and standardized each gene across that

378 donor's cells using the population standard deviation. A gene constant within that donor was

379 represented by a zero centered vector. We fitted Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage covariance [39] to this

380 matrix with an assumed zero mean, converted the covariance to a correlation matrix, and

381 averaged the five donor matrices with equal coefficients. This procedure prevents a donor with

382 thousands of cells from entering as thousands of independent biological replicates. Donor-

383 specific shrinkage nevertheless regularizes estimates according to their information content, and

384 an unshrunk Pearson-correlation ablation was fitted to measure its effect. Covariance

385 regularization follows established high-dimensional network practice [43]; the implemented

386 estimator was Ledoit–Wolf, not an alternative shrinkage algorithm.

387 The primary graph retained positive correlation weights and the 20 strongest neighbors of each

418 Expression-matched nulls and graph-degree sensitivity

419 For each compartment, background genes excluded the 13 seeds and the 17 target candidates.

420 Each seed was matched to its 50 nearest background genes using Euclidean distance in

421 standardized log(1 + equal-donor mean expression) and logit detection, with a 0.005

422 pseudocount applied symmetrically to detection and non-detection. We drew 1,999 null seed sets

423 with random seed 20260919. Within a null set, a matched gene could be used only once, and

425 remained fixed, so its own network degree was shared by the observed and null propagation

427 gene universe; it is not a population-randomization test.

432 clinical-weight profile. If the null variance was zero, Z was recorded as undefined, rather than

527 Fixed aggregation rules, null calibration, and sensitivity analysis

528 We compared the exact earlier median-rank consensus with five aggregation rules applied to the

529 revised matrix: median rank, arithmetic mean, geometric mean, maximin, and an order-statistic

530 score based on robust rank aggregation. The earlier consensus was reconstructed from source-

531 specific descending average ranks, their median, and the mean rank used to break median-rank

532 ties; its 17 published ranks were reproduced exactly. This comparison distinguishes the historical

533 workflow from comparisons among alternative fusion rules using identical revised inputs.

534 For each revised feature j, a target's increasing average rank rtj was converted to the midpoint

535 percentile xtj = (rtj − 0.5)/nj, where nj was the number of observed targets for that feature.

536 Higher values denoted greater relative support. The primary fusion rule was the equal-weight

537 geometric mean of the four percentiles, exp[sumj log(xtj)/4]. Arithmetic fusion used their mean,

538 maximin used their minimum, and revised median fusion used their median with 10−7 times the

539 mean as a deterministic tiebreaker. Candidate output ranks used descending average ranks. This

540 rule was selected to require balanced relative support, before assessing which algorithm

541 performed best on the available diagnostics. It was not optimized to favor any named protein. An

542 unobserved feature was assigned a conservative percentile of 0.5/17 in the primary calculation;

543 neutral-percentile and observed-feature-only analyses quantified sensitivity to that choice.

544 Directly measured HepG2 landmarks were also evaluated alone, with all inferred values masked

545 together. Missingness was never described as a negative biological response.

1448 A connected experimental model emerges from the public-data analysis

1449 The complete analysis links a treatment-limiting hepatic safety concern to a measurable human

1450 protein phenotype and to experimentally accessible pharmacological candidates. FBP1, GSTA1,

1451 and OTC define complementary injury measurements within the wider 13-protein program.

1452 PDGFRA and FLT4 are the most stable relative priorities across the tested fusion rules and

1453 weight perturbations; their ranking is a decision about the next intervention experiments, not a

1454 claim that either receptor causes DILI. The independent hepatic drug-exposure analysis and

1455 pulmonary models assess whether the proposed interventions can be interpreted in a tissue

1456 context. Together, these results specify a falsifiable mechanism: perturbation of drug-responsive

1457 non-parenchymal signaling may alter hepatocyte metabolic resilience under nintedanib exposure.

1882 Supplementary protocol: target confirmation and liver–lung therapeutic-window testing

1883 This protocol specifies follow-up experiments motivated by the completed public-data analyses.

1884 It has not been executed or registered and contributes no observed result to this paper. Its

1885 purpose is to test the proposed sequence from drug-binding protein to a cellular intermediate and

1886 then to the same human injury proteins used in the computational analysis.


1887 Biological question and candidate order

1888 The initial candidates are PDGFRA and FLT4, followed by FGFR2, JAK1, and FGFR3. KDR is

1889 included as a hepatic pharmacodynamic comparator and FGFR1 as the comparator affected by




                                                       83
1890 numerical recalibration. CES1 provides a drug-disposition control. Candidate ranking does not

1891 specify whether inhibition or activation should be protective. A candidate is advanced only after

1892 reproducible target engagement, an exposure-matched effect on hepatic injury, reciprocal rescue,

1893 and retention of nintedanib activity in a pulmonary model.

1894 PDGFRA should first be tested in verified PDGFRA-positive primary human hepatic stellate

1895 cells, rather than macrophages selected solely from the sparse transcriptomic network signal.

1896 FLT4 should be tested in verified FLT4-positive liver sinusoidal endothelial cells. Surface

1897 protein, transcript detection, ligand response, and receptor phosphorylation will establish

1898 whether the intended cell preparation supports the intervention. A preparation without

1899 measurable functional receptor is an assay-context failure, not a negative test of a receptor-

1900 mediated mechanism.


1901 Exposure and donor design

1902 The exploratory phase will use primary human hepatocytes from eight independent donors, with

1903 both sexes represented where feasible, and independent endothelial or stellate preparations. A

1904 second hepatic platform will use donor-derived organoids or multicellular microtissues. Where

1905 non-parenchymal and hepatocyte samples cannot be obtained from the same donor, both

1906 identities will be recorded and pairing will be accounted for in the model. Technical wells will be

1907 averaged within the biological unit.

1908 A concentration pilot will measure free medium and intracellular nintedanib over 6, 24, 48, and

1909 72 h. The initial concentration range will span 0.01–1.11 µM, with 3.33 and 10 µM used only as

1910 high-exposure comparators to the LINCS experiment. No in-vitro nominal concentration will be

1911 described as clinically equivalent without accounting for protein binding, intracellular

1912 accumulation, and the clinical exposure distribution. Vehicle content will be identical across




                                                       84
1913 conditions. Validated LC–MS/MS will quantify parent nintedanib, BIBF 1202, and BIBF 1202

1914 glucuronide in medium and lysates. Changes in metabolites or transport will be assessed before

1915 attributing rescue to improved cellular tolerance.


1916 Perturbation and reciprocal rescue

1917 Each candidate will be tested using at least two independent inducible CRISPR-interference or

1918 siRNA reagents, a non-targeting control, an orthogonal gain-of-function condition, and a

1919 reagent-resistant wild-type rescue construct. For receptors, a kinase-inactive rescue will test

1920 dependence on kinase activity. Perturbations will be crossed with vehicle and nintedanib

1921 exposure. Ligand controls will be matched to the actual receptor being tested; receptor

1922 engagement and proximal phosphorylation will be measured in the same experiment.

1923 We will compare cell monoculture, conditioned-medium transfer, transwell co-culture, and direct

1924 multicellular culture. This sequence separates cell-autonomous effects from secreted and contact-

1925 dependent interactions. Hepatocyte viability, extracellular ALT and LDH, albumin production,

1926 urea production, and apoptosis/necrosis imaging will be measured alongside intracellular and

1927 extracellular FBP1, GSTA1, OTC, and the remaining available human protein panel. Targeted

1928 mass spectrometry or validated immunoassays will distinguish protein abundance from enzyme

1929 activity. The circulating protein phenotype is an outcome to be explained; it is not assumed to be

1930 a therapeutic target.

1931 Candidate intermediates will be measured only after a reproducible perturbation effect is

1932 established. Sampling before overt injury will assess receptor phosphorylation, ERK/AKT

1933 signaling, endothelial barrier function, angiocrine secretion, hepatocyte energy state,

1934 mitochondrial respiration, redox balance, and nitrogen handling. An intermediate becomes a

1935 mechanism candidate when it changes before injury, is altered by both independent target




                                                       85
1936 reagents, and mediates a rescue when restored or blocked. Broad phosphoproteomics and

1937 secretome profiling may identify additional intermediates, but these discovery analyses will be

1938 separated from the subsequent confirmatory experiment.


1939 Distinguishing drug disposition from tolerance

1940 CES1 loss and gain of function will measure how changes in hydrolysis alter parent-drug and

1941 metabolite exposure. Candidate receptor perturbations will be evaluated at matched intracellular

1942 parent exposure whenever feasible. A reduction in injury caused by lower intracellular

1943 nintedanib will be interpreted as a disposition effect. A tolerance mechanism requires lower

1944 injury at comparable intracellular exposure, supported by both a concentration-matched analysis

1945 and the prespecified exposure-adjusted model. Extracellular protein reduction without preserved

1946 viability, metabolic function, or intracellular protein activity will not constitute rescue.


1947 Preserving pulmonary benefit

1948 The same candidate intervention will be tested with nintedanib in primary fibrotic-ILD

1949 fibroblasts and human precision-cut lung slices. TGF-β1-induced COL1A1, COL1A2, ACTA2,

1950 FN1, procollagen release, matrix contraction, and collagen deposition will supply pulmonary

1951 endpoints. The pilot will establish a nintedanib-responsive condition before evaluating the

1952 interaction. Models with a weak baseline drug effect cannot support a meaningful claim of

1953 efficacy preservation.

1954 For the confirmatory study, a non-inferiority margin will be fixed from biological relevance and

1955 assay reproducibility before unblinding. A provisional margin of no more than 20% loss of the

1956 nintedanib effect will be evaluated during design, not adopted as an observed result. Both the co-

1957 primary collagen endpoints and tissue viability must support preservation. Drug and metabolite

1958 measurements will determine whether a liver-sparing intervention changes pulmonary exposure.




                                                        86
1959 In-vivo confirmation

1960 Only candidates passing the human-cell tests will enter a randomized, blinded factorial

1961 experiment with vehicle, nintedanib, candidate intervention, and combination. The pulmonary

1962 disease model must show a measurable nintedanib response in a preliminary qualification

1963 experiment. Both sexes will be included. Animal numbers will be determined by simulation from

1964 independent pilot variance components for the candidate-by-drug interaction; no number is

1965 claimed as already powered.

1966 Lung endpoints will include blinded fibrosis histology, hydroxyproline, lung mechanics, and the

1967 relevant matrix proteins. Liver endpoints will include ALT/AST, bilirubin, bile acids, blinded

1968 histology, the circulating and hepatic protein panel, and tissue drug exposure. Allocation

1969 concealment, humane endpoints, exclusion rules, and a statistical analysis plan will be registered

1970 before enrollment. A bleomycin model is not assumed to reproduce idiosyncratic human DILI;

1971 the experiment tests a defined mechanism and a liver–lung treatment window.


1972 Statistical decision rules

1973 The biological donor or animal is the inferential unit. Treatment order and plate position will be

1974 randomized, and imaging, pathology, and analysis will be blinded. The primary in-vitro model

1975 will include drug, perturbation, and their interaction, with donor-level effects and the necessary

1976 crossed donor terms for multicellular preparations. Concentration dependence will be evaluated

1977 using a prespecified smooth function of log exposure. Technical failures will be recorded with

1978 reasons and will not be imputed to improve a result.

1979 Confirmatory co-primary liver endpoints will be controlled with Holm correction. Secondary

1980 molecular families will use BH correction with effect sizes and confidence intervals. Candidates

1981 must show concordant effects with two reagents, reciprocal rescue, replication in the second




                                                      87
1982 hepatic platform, and no meaningful loss of pulmonary activity. Discovery donors will not be

1983 reused as confirmatory biological replicates. These requirements are prospective decision

1984 criteria; no candidate has yet satisfied them in this study.
