## Matched experimental benchmark

Same-study dissociation constants (Kd) from Davis et al. (2011), shown separately for PDGFRA and FLT4. Circles denote PDGFRA and squares FLT4. Filled symbols identify records that pass the strict current database eligibility flags; open symbols retain source-matched records marked as potential duplicates in ChEMBL. Each of the 12 source records is displayed once; no sample pooling or inferential test is performed. The imatinib FLT4 value remains right-censored at >10,000 nM. The source benchmark contains six compounds, rather than the complete comparator library. These values are experimental biochemical measurements from the source study and are not new experiments.

## Full molecular-property predictions

Predicted logD at pH 7.4, acidic pKa and basic pKa for the 16 prespecified parent structures. The index drug is highlighted. Points are provider model estimates and segments are provider-returned model bounds, whose coverage was unspecified; they are not confidence intervals. Open symbols denote out-of-domain flags, and diamonds denote low-confidence predictions. The models are mcp_public_logd version 1.7.0 and mcp_public_apka/mcp_public_bpka version 1.4.0. All structures matched the verified input identity. Property values do not establish pharmacokinetics, DILI risk, or hepatoprotection.

## Endpoint evidence coverage

Numbers are eligible exact-relation biochemical activity records under the prespecified source filters, separated by receptor and Kd, Ki or IC50. A dash indicates that no eligible record was available in the extraction; it does not indicate inactivity. Human-target mapping and absence of a stated assay variant do not establish that the construct was explicitly documented as human wild type. Full record dispositions and contextual experimental evidence are retained in the candidate-evidence tables.

## Complete docking campaign (final counts gated)

(A,B) Actual scores from all 16 compounds under each of three prespecified search seeds, shown separately by receptor. PDGFRA used the experimental imatinib-bound 6JOL receptor; FLT4 used a predicted AlphaFold receptor. Color scales and scores are interpreted within each receptor. (C,D) Maximum pairwise, receptor-frame, symmetry-aware heavy-atom RMSD among the three top poses per compound, if confirmed by the executing analysis implementation. (E) Endpoint-specific biochemical record coverage. (F) All 30 retained PDGFRA nintedanib poses across the three prespecified seeds, with dark-outlined symbols identifying each top-ranked pose and distances measured to Thr674, the gatekeeper residue. The dashed 4 Å line is a proximity reference and does not define a hydrogen bond or canonical hinge interaction. Repeated seeds assess stochastic search consistency and are not biological replicates; no significance test is performed across seeds. Neither docking score nor computational binding confidence is a calibrated experimental affinity or evidence of a beneficial intervention direction.

## Figure 7: Experimental binding and model-based comparator profiles

(A) Same-study experimental dissociation constants from Davis et al. (2011), displayed separately for PDGFRA and FLT4. Open symbols mark source-matched, potential-duplicate-flagged ChEMBL records; every source record is shown once, and the imatinib FLT4 observation remains censored at >10,000 nM. (B,C) Target-specific BoltzMol binding-confidence scores from completed pipelineboltzmol version 1.0 runs. Twelve compounds per receptor received scores; nintedanib, sunitinib, ponatinib and MAZ51 were filtered by the provider's default structural-alert settings. Filtered entries have no numerical score and are not demonstrated nonbinders. Binding confidence is a dimensionless provider score, not an experimentally measured affinity or calibrated probability. (D) Imatinib crystal and independently redocked structures from the separate method-control run in the unchanged 6JOL receptor coordinate frame; the symmetry-aware heavy-atom RMSD is 0.574 Å, without ligand superposition. The three primary-library imatinib searches independently gave crystal-pose RMSDs of 0.556, 0.776 and 0.712 Å. (E,F) BoltzMol-predicted imatinib–PDGFRA and tivozanib–FLT4 pocket structures. The exported models are pocket-limited, containing 199 of 362 PDGFRA input residues and 195 of 329 FLT4 input residues; displayed residue numbers are mapped to canonical protein positions. Annotated distances are heavy-atom proximities, not hydrogen-bond assignments. The chemical identities and model-coordinate provenance were verified. Pirfenidone and nerandomilast are clinical nonkinase references rather than validated biochemical nonbinders. These comparisons prioritize mechanistic experiments and do not establish clinical safety, hepatoprotection, or a beneficial intervention direction.
