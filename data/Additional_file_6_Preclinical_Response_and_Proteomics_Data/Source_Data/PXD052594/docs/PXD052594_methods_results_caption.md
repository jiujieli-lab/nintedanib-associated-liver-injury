# PXD052594 animal-level reanalysis: frozen Methods, Results, and Figure S7 legend

## Source and evidence boundary

PXD052594 and the linked reproduction deposit (Zenodo record 11395642; doi:10.5281/zenodo.11395642) accompany Lauer et al., *JCI Insight* 2024;9(15):e181757 (PMID 39012714; doi:10.1172/jci.insight.181757). The source experiment used 8-week-old female C57BL/6J mice given bleomycin 2 U/kg intratracheally and randomized double-blind to nintedanib 60 mg/kg orally once daily or vehicle on days 7–20. Animals were killed on day 21, 24 hours after the final dose, and the middle right lung lobe was used for proteomics. Of 30 randomized mice (15 per arm), three reached humane endpoints and three had severe CT abnormalities; the resulting radiomic cohort contained 10 nintedanib and 14 vehicle mice. One additional vehicle sample was excluded upstream from comparative proteomics because of sample-preparation problems, leaving the public processed protein matrix with 10 nintedanib and 13 vehicle animals. These postrandomization exclusions require a sensitivity rather than unbiased causal interpretation. The analysis is a pulmonary pharmacodynamic constraint and contains no liver injury measurements.

## Methods

The deposited `proteomics_log2` worksheet contained complete processed log2 abundances for 7,006 proteins in 23 individual mice (10 nintedanib; 13 vehicle). Mouse Entrez identifiers were mapped to current symbols with the NCBI *Mus musculus* `gene_info` file downloaded on 4 September 2026. We compared every remaining nintedanib animal with every remaining vehicle animal; post-treatment radiomic response clusters were not used to select, exclude, stratify, adjust, or label any sample. Protein log2 fold changes were differences in animal means. Two-sided Welch t tests were BH-corrected across all 7,006 proteins. Hedges' *g* and Welch 95% confidence intervals were calculated for prespecified tracks.

Five protein programs were prespecified: extracellular-matrix remodeling, macrophage repair, inflammation, alveolar homeostasis, and receptor-tyrosine-kinase response. Each protein was standardized across the 23 animals and available members were averaged per animal. Module contrasts used Welch t tests, exact two-sided Mann–Whitney tests, and 100,000 seeded treatment-label permutations; BH correction was applied across the five modules for each test family. Fgfr1 was the prespecified exposure-proximal target track displayed in this lung dataset. Fbp1 was a downstream P1 DILI phenotype anchor and Gsta1/Otc were downstream P2 phenotype anchors; none of these phenotype anchors was interpreted as a drug target. Principal-component analysis used the 2,000 most variable proteins.

The separate deposited phosphoproteome comprised 20,043 complete iMaxLFQ phosphosite rows in a random subset of five nintedanib and five vehicle animals. We log2-transformed the deposited positive intensities, compared all five animals per arm with two-sided Welch tests, and controlled BH FDR across all 20,043 sites. The phosphosite analysis was not conditioned on radiomic clusters and is provided as a source-table sensitivity only, not as a Figure S7 panel. Exact software versions, input/output hashes, and a detached checksum validation are stored in the analysis manifest.

## Results

The unconditioned 23-animal lung-proteome contrast yielded no protein at BH q<0.10 (minimum q=0.608), despite complete measurement of all 7,006 protein rows (Figure S7A–D). None of the five prespecified programs met BH significance. The extracellular-matrix score was lower by 0.33062 with nintedanib (95% CI, −0.86329 to 0.20205; Hedges' *g*=−0.544; Welch P=0.208; 100,000-permutation P=0.196; permutation-BH q=0.480). Macrophage-repair, inflammatory, alveolar-homeostasis, and RTK-response estimates were also multiplicity-null (all Welch-BH q≥0.494; all permutation-BH q≥0.480; Figure S7E–H).

Fgfr1 protein abundance was similar between groups (log2 fold change 0.11803; 95% CI, −0.42027 to 0.65633; *g*=0.188; Welch P=0.651; detected-track BH q=0.760). The Fbp1 P1 phenotype anchor and Gsta1/Otc P2 phenotype anchors were not quantified in the lung-proteome matrix. None of the displayed lung phenotype tracks survived BH correction (Figure S7I,J).

In contrast to protein abundance, the separate 5-versus-5 phosphoproteome sensitivity produced 994 of 20,043 sites at BH q<0.05 in an unmoderated Welch–BH screen (495 lower and 499 higher with nintedanib; 983 also had absolute log2 fold change at least 1). This broad, bidirectional, model-dependent screen is not an exact small-sample test and does not establish signaling-state heterogeneity: only deposited processed intensities were available, and no matched total-protein abundance adjustment or site-occupancy model was applied, so phosphosite shifts cannot be separated from corresponding protein-abundance shifts. Given the small subset, the result is not used as causal target validation. None of the quantified canonical RTK-pathway sites in Fgfr3, Pdgfra, Flt4, Mapk1, Mapk3, Akt1, or Stat3 met phosphosite BH q<0.05; Fgfr1 phosphosites were not quantified. The full site table and prespecified pathway summary are supplied for auditability.

Together, these findings show that a group-wide protein-abundance response was not recovered in the unconditioned contrast, whereas the unmoderated phosphosite screen contains widespread deposited differences that do not specifically validate the manuscript's target/phenotype chain. PXD052594 is therefore retained as a high-value but nonconfirmatory pulmonary-response sensitivity, not evidence of hepatic toxicity or an unbiased causal treatment effect.
