# Public preclinical perturbation analysis: manuscript-ready Methods and Results

## Methods

### Systematic resource discovery and eligibility

We searched GEO, SRA/ENA, DDBJ, ArrayExpress/BioStudies, PRIDE/ProteomeXchange, MetaboLights, Open TG-GATEs, DrugMatrix, ToxCast/Tox21, LINCS, and the JUMP/Cell Painting collection through 4 September 2026 using *nintedanib*, *BIBF 1120*, *lung*, *pulmonary fibrosis*, *bleomycin*, *liver*, *hepatic*, *hepatocyte*, *toxicity*, *proteome*, *transcriptome*, and *metabolome* in Boolean combinations. An experiment was eligible when it provided a downloadable raw-count or processed abundance matrix, an unambiguous nintedanib dose and exposure interval, a concurrent disease/vehicle control, and recoverable sample-level provenance. We required an organ or model context that directly informed either pulmonary antifibrotic response or hepatic exposure. Lung experiments were used only to define a pulmonary-response compatibility constraint; they were not interpreted as evidence for or against liver injury. The complete search ledger, including negative searches and exclusions, is provided in `dataset_screening_ledger.csv`.

### Experimental-unit adjudication

The experimental unit was established from the repository metadata and the original Methods before any statistical testing. GSE278200 comprised 12 independently sampled male Sprague–Dawley rat right lungs (SAL, bleomycin [BLM], and BLM plus nintedanib; four animals per group). BLM was administered intratracheally at 1 U/kg on days 0 and 4; nintedanib was administered orally at 100 mg/kg once daily from day 7 through day 28. GSE308578 comprised 43 independently sampled male C57BL/6J mouse inferior lung lobes (saline/vehicle control, n=10; BLM/vehicle, n=16; BLM/nintedanib, n=17). In this study, BLM was administered intratracheally at 2 mg/kg and nintedanib at 60 mg/kg orally twice daily from day 8 through day 28. GEO SOFT and the source article's ARRIVE design table (Supplementary Figure S1) both specify 10/16/17 animals; the source article's main Figure 6 caption reverses the vehicle and nintedanib counts. We therefore retained the GEO/ARRIVE labels without reassigning any sample and recorded this source-level discrepancy in the quality audit.

The two precision-cut tissue-slice experiments were not treated as independent-animal studies. For GSE120804, three sham- or BDL-operated rat livers were used per ex vivo study, slices from the same surgical procedure were pooled, and the pooled slices were randomized to treatment. The relevant processed-matrix columns were sham (9 slice observations), BDL/vehicle (10), and BDL plus 1.0 µM nintedanib for 48 h (10). For GSE120679, slices from three rat lungs were pooled before randomization; the relevant groups were vehicle at 72 h, TGFβ1 at 72 h, and TGFβ1 plus 0.3 µM nintedanib at 72 h (six slice observations per group). Consequently, PCLS statistics quantify within-pool slice reproducibility only and are explicitly labelled exploratory; they do not estimate an animal-population effect. In PXD024058, the public Methods state that three lung tissues per group were selected for TMT labelling, whereas the Results call the three quantitative columns technical replicates. Those columns were therefore used for descriptive direction checks without p values, confidence intervals, or biological-replicate claims.

### RNA-seq preprocessing and animal-level differential expression

Raw counts for GSE278200 and GSE308578 were mapped from stable Ensembl identifiers to gene symbols using the matching rat Ensembl release 103 annotation and the current mouse GRCm39 Ensembl annotation, respectively. Duplicate symbols were collapsed by summing counts. Genes were retained when counts per million were at least 1 in at least the size of the smallest experimental group (four animals for GSE278200 and ten for GSE308578). Library composition was normalized by the trimmed mean of M values (TMM; 30% M-value trimming and 5% absolute-expression trimming), and log2 counts per million were calculated with a 0.5-count prior for visualization and modelling.

For each independent-animal dataset, we fitted a saturated group-means linear model to TMM logCPM. Residual variances were moderated with a robust empirical-Bayes prior: a LOWESS mean–variance trend was fitted to log residual variance as a function of mean expression; extreme log-variance residuals were winsorized at the 5th and 95th percentiles; and the scaled-F prior degrees of freedom and trend-dependent prior variance were estimated by moments. Two-sided moderated *t* statistics were calculated for disease versus control, nintedanib versus disease/vehicle, and nintedanib versus control. Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied across all retained genes separately within each contrast. Reported confidence intervals are 95% moderated-*t* intervals. No sample was removed after inspecting the results.

### Prespecified target and pathway tests

The target/anchor panel was specified before inspecting effects: **FGFR1, FGFR3, FLT1, OTC, GSTA1, FBP1**, and the rat/mouse CES1 orthologue **CES1D**. Context genes included **CPS1, ASS1, ARG1, COL1A1, ACTA2, POSTN, FN1, CTGF**, and **ABCB1B**. Seven gene modules were likewise specified a priori: ECM/fibrosis, macrophage–lipid handling, urea/arginine metabolism, nintedanib target track, hepatic metabolic anchors, oxidative stress, and drug efflux. Within each dataset, each gene was standardized across samples and module scores were the unweighted mean of available standardized genes. Animal-level module effects were tested by label permutation. For GSE278200, all 70 possible allocations of four of eight animals were enumerated for each pairwise contrast. For GSE308578, 50,000 random label permutations were used with a +1 correction. Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied across the seven modules within each contrast. Effect bars show Welch 95% intervals; permutation *q* values govern module-level inference.

For visualization of treatment reversal, disease-responsive genes were defined by disease-versus-control *q*<0.05 and |log2 fold change|≥0.5. Directional reversal required opposite signs for disease and treatment effects. The reversal index was defined as minus the treatment effect divided by the disease effect; 0 indicates no reversal, 1 complete return to the control mean, and values above 1 overshoot. Because genes are correlated, the effect-space correlation and reversal fractions are descriptive summaries, not gene-as-independent-unit hypothesis tests. GSE308578 robustness was additionally evaluated by omitting each of the 33 BLM/vehicle or BLM/nintedanib animals in turn and recalculating prespecified gene and module effects.

### Pooled-slice and proteomic analyses

GSE120804 raw counts were TMM normalized and transformed to logCPM using the same expression filter, while the authors' protein-coding logCPM matrix was used for GSE120679. Pooled-slice contrasts used two-sided Welch statistics with genome-wide Benjamini–Hochberg correction, but every p and q value is labelled `slice_exploratory`. Probability-of-superiority effect sizes were also retained. These calculations do not convert pooled slices into independent animals. PXD024058 protein ratios were log2 transformed, duplicate gene symbols were collapsed by their median, and the difference between the mean model-plus-nintedanib and model log2 ratios was reported descriptively. Cross-model heat-map module values are means of the available gene-level log2 effects, not separately tested enrichment statistics.

All analyses used Python 3.12 with NumPy, pandas, SciPy, statsmodels, scikit-learn, and Matplotlib; exact versions and SHA-256 checksums are frozen in `results/analysis_manifest.json`. The random seed was 20260904.

## Results

### Independent rat lung data show a pulmonary transcriptional response to nintedanib

In GSE278200, BLM produced a broad lung transcriptomic shift: 5,977 of 13,598 expressed genes met genome-wide *q*<0.05, and 3,647 additionally had |log2 fold change|≥0.5. Nintedanib altered 895 genes at *q*<0.05 (551 with |log2 fold change|≥0.5) relative to BLM. Among the 3,647 disease-responsive genes, 94.4% changed in the opposite direction after nintedanib and 94.4% were closer to the saline mean. The median reversal index among directionally reversed genes was 0.514, and 51.5% had a reversal index from 0.5 to 1.5. This genome-wide geometry therefore indicated substantial but incomplete restoration, while remaining descriptive because genes are correlated.

At the prespecified fibrosis loci, nintedanib reduced **COL1A1** (log2 fold change −1.607, 95% CI −2.508 to −0.705, genome-wide *q*=0.0404) and **POSTN** (−0.467, 95% CI −0.660 to −0.275, *q*=0.0188). The ECM/fibrosis module shifted by −1.156 standardized units. Its exact permutation *p*=0.0286, the smallest attainable two-sided value with four versus four animals, but correction across seven modules yielded *q*=0.200. This distinction is important: coherent ECM reversal and two genome-wide significant fibrosis genes support a pulmonary-response constraint, whereas the small experiment does not establish module-wide efficacy at multiplicity-controlled significance. **FGFR3** was below the expression filter; **FGFR1** (+0.253, *q*=0.682), **FLT1** (+0.227, *q*=0.234), **OTC** (−0.153, *q*=0.815), **GSTA1** (−0.208, *q*=0.356), and **FBP1** (−0.640, *q*=0.345) were not individually significant. Accordingly, a prospective safety-directed intervention should be tested for retention of pulmonary antifibrotic activity without assuming that every candidate must change transcriptionally in whole lung.

### An independent mouse study defines a model-specific efficacy boundary and an efflux response

GSE308578 provided a deliberately discordant, higher-powered model. BLM changed 8,418 of 14,305 expressed genes at genome-wide *q*<0.05 (3,499 with |log2 fold change|≥0.5), whereas nintedanib changed 1,059 genes relative to BLM/vehicle (208 with |log2 fold change|≥0.5). Although 78.9% of the 3,499 disease-responsive genes moved in the opposite direction, the median reversal index was only 0.257 and only 14.2% of directionally reversed genes reached an index of 0.5–1.5. Consistent with this lower-magnitude transcriptomic restoration, neither ECM/fibrosis (effect −0.108 standardized units; 50,000-permutation *q*=0.793) nor macrophage–lipid handling (−0.095; *q*=0.793) was detectably shifted, and the nintedanib-target module was also null (+0.043; *q*=0.793).

In contrast, **ABCB1B**, which encodes the P-glycoprotein efflux transporter, increased by 0.519 log2 units (95% CI 0.271–0.767; genome-wide *q*=0.00788). The direction remained positive in every leave-one-animal-out analysis (range 0.461–0.580). The drug-efflux module increased by 0.463 standardized units (permutation *p*=0.00958; seven-module *q*=0.0671). These results reproduce the source study's model-boundary interpretation: nintedanib engaged a measurable pulmonary transcriptional response, including robust **ABCB1B** induction, without materially reversing the core ECM or macrophage–lipid programmes or the reported physiological and histological phenotype. The model therefore constrains, rather than invalidates, the positive efficacy signal from GSE278200.

### Hepatic PCLS perturbation demonstrates strong context dependence rather than DILI recapitulation

In pooled BDL liver PCLSs (GSE120804), 1.0 µM nintedanib for 48 h produced large within-pool slice-level shifts in several study candidates: **FGFR3** +0.724 (slice-level *q*=8.47×10−7), **FLT1** −1.645 (*q*=9.08×10−6), **OTC** +1.590 (*q*=0.00121), **FBP1** +1.818 (*q*=7.16×10−4), and **CES1D** +1.251 (*q*=1.18×10−4). **GSTA1** was unchanged (−0.102, *q*=0.445). Nintedanib also reduced **COL1A1** (−0.919, *q*=2.39×10−5), **POSTN** (−1.259, *q*=3.19×10−6), and **FN1** (−1.162, *q*=9.67×10−9), while the pooled-slice hepatic-anchor score increased (+1.051 standardized units; exploratory module *q*=3.47×10−6). Because the tissues had been pooled before treatment allocation, these q values measure reproducibility among slices from one pooled biological source. They neither demonstrate population-level liver effects nor model drug-induced liver injury. Directionally, the increases in **OTC** and **FBP1** oppose a simple model in which nintedanib directly suppresses these proteins in every hepatic state, thereby identifying cholestatic context as an important modifier of the candidate mechanism.

### Descriptive cross-model data separate conserved target engagement from organ-specific metabolic effects

The pooled lung PCLS experiment (GSE120679) showed the same target-track direction as liver PCLS for **FGFR3** (+0.766; slice-level *q*=0.0574) and **FLT1** (−0.834; *q*=0.0115), although only **FLT1** survived genome-wide slice-level correction. No ECM-module shift was detected (+0.026 standardized units; exploratory module *q*=0.760). In the PXD024058 mouse-lung TMT table, nintedanib-versus-BLM effects were −1.285 for OTC, −1.055 for FBP1, −1.650 for CPS1, −0.791 for ASS1, and −0.278 for COL1A1. These protein changes are direction checks only because the three columns per group have unresolved biological-versus-technical provenance.

Together, the public perturbation layer defines a two-sided translational constraint. Nintedanib was associated with partial reversal of a BLM-driven lung programme in independent rats, but the response was model dependent and attenuated in an independent mouse experiment characterized by **ABCB1B** induction. In hepatic BDL slices, candidate metabolic anchors responded strongly but in a direction that did not recapitulate the human DILI signature. These public experiments therefore motivate prospective testing of FGFR/FLT1 target-track and OTC/GSTA1/FBP1/CES1 metabolic nodes while preventing two overinterpretations: lung response datasets are not liver-safety evidence, and pooled liver slices are not an animal model of nintedanib-induced liver injury.
