## Methods — public human DILI proteomic phenotype anchor

We independently re-analysed the publicly released source-data workbook from the multicentre serum-proteomics study by Ravindra et al. (Nature Communications 2023; DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-36858-6; source-file SHA-256: `5911fe3d24efa1b639000e5c9176ef9cee7a44b4bed7ff75fe818ee1d6ef6b16`). The discovery cohort comprised healthy volunteers (HV, n=10), adjudicated drug-induced liver injury at onset and follow-up (DO and DF, n=10 each), acute non-drug liver injury at onset and follow-up (NDO and NDF, n=5 each), and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease controls (n=10). We extracted the variance-stabilizing-normalized TMT intensities of 12 prespecified liver-enriched candidates from Figure 2C and LECT2 from Supplementary Figure 1. For the confirmatory cohort, Figure 3 was the canonical source for HV, DO and NDO values because it explicitly declared group sizes (60, 82 and 34, respectively); Supplementary Figure 2 supplied the available DF/NDF series, and Supplementary Figure 3 supplied PCK2 values. Biomarker–ALT pairs were extracted only where both measurements were given in the same source row (Figure 5 and Supplementary Figure 7). Figure 6 liver-zone scores were converted to long format, and the second, exact copy of the 10 HV observations per zone was removed.

All source values were retained as text and parsed under a frozen rule set. Explicit `ND` entries remained missing and were never recoded as zero; the unexplained starred PCK2 value (`3077.0266*`) was retained with an outlier flag. Primary two-group comparisons used a two-sided Mann–Whitney test and Cliff’s delta, with 95% percentile intervals from 2,000 group-stratified bootstrap samples. Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied within each cohort and contrast across the candidate proteins. We also report median differences, confirmatory median ratios, and, secondarily, Welch tests and Hedges’ g after protein-specific log2 transformation. Apparent univariate ROC AUCs were bootstrapped at the participant-observation level. Differential non-detection was tested by Fisher’s exact test; confirmatory analyses were repeated after substituting one-half of the protein-specific minimum positive value for ND observations. Spearman correlations with ALT were estimated in pooled HV+DO observations and separately within DO and HV, with bootstrap confidence intervals and FDR control. The within-DO analysis was prespecified to distinguish severity association from correlation induced by case–control separation.

The workbook did not provide identifiers linking measurements across proteins or linking onset to follow-up. Moreover, cross-figure QC demonstrated that several identical protein value sets appeared in different row orders. We therefore did not create a positional participant-by-protein matrix, fit a multivariable classifier, perform nested cross-validation, or treat onset and follow-up values as paired. These analyses require an official de-identified sample manifest or participant-level matrix. Recovery contrasts were analysed as unpaired and considered exploratory because Supplementary Figure 2 column lengths differed from the cohort sizes reported in the article.

## Results — FBP1 recapitulates an etiologic component among source-selected DILI candidates

The source workbook yielded protein-specific individual observations for 13 candidates selected by the source study but no linkable multivariable participant matrix. Eleven candidates showed at least moderate separation of DO from HV after FDR control (absolute Cliff’s delta ≥0.33): ACO1, ALDOB, ASS1, CES1, CPS1, DMGDH, FAH, FBP1, GSTA1, HPD and OTC. ACO1 produced the largest effect (delta=0.978, 95% CI 0.950–0.996; q=3.50×10⁻²²), followed by ASS1 (delta=0.967, 95% CI 0.934–0.990; q=5.80×10⁻²²) and FAH (delta=0.954, 95% CI 0.894–0.993; q=1.49×10⁻²¹). These findings recapitulate a broad circulating hepatocellular-injury signature within the released candidate panel but do not constitute an unbiased proteome-wide discovery or distinguish drug causation from other acute liver injury.

FBP1 was the only candidate that remained different between DO and NDO after multiplicity correction. FBP1 was lower in DO (median 0.00770; 76/82 detected) than NDO (median 0.02075; 32/34 detected), with Cliff’s delta of −0.505 (95% CI −0.724 to −0.288; p=3.61×10⁻⁵; q=4.69×10⁻⁴). With the higher NDO concentration specified as the positive direction, the apparent univariate AUC was 0.753 (95% CI 0.644–0.862). The association persisted when ND observations were replaced by one-half of the minimum positive value (delta=−0.455; q=0.00156). LECT2 and PCK2 reached nominal p<0.05 in the complete-case comparison but did not survive FDR correction (q=0.175 and 0.161, respectively); all remaining candidates had q≥0.206. Thus, only FBP1 provided FDR-controlled evidence for the etiologic DILI-versus-non-DILI component, whereas most other candidates tracked liver injury burden.

FBP1 also separated DO from HV (HV median 0.00040; 37/60 detected): delta=0.922 (95% CI 0.847–0.978; q=4.02×10⁻¹⁵) and apparent AUC=0.961 (95% CI 0.923–0.989). Differential detection between DO and HV was substantial and FDR-significant, so the magnitude of this comparison includes both abundance and censoring components. Across the 12 candidates with evaluable, non-duplicated discovery values, the DO–NDO direction agreed descriptively between discovery and confirmatory cohorts for 11 proteins (91.7%), and the descriptive Spearman correlation of effect magnitudes was 0.561. We do not attach cross-protein binomial or correlation P values to these summaries because the proteins are correlated features measured within the same source cohorts rather than independent sampling units. HPD was excluded from this cross-cohort summary because all 50 discovery HPD entries were an exact cell-for-cell duplicate of ALDOB, indicating an unresolved source-workbook anomaly.

Nine of 11 candidates with usable follow-up source series—ACO1, ALDOB, ASS1, CPS1, DMGDH, FAH, GSTA1, HPD and OTC—were lower at DILI follow-up than onset with FDR-significant, moderate-to-large rank effects. LECT2 and PCK2 did not change materially. Because follow-up identifiers were absent and the supplementary column counts were inconsistent with the published cohort totals, these results support recovery association but not within-patient kinetics. FBP1 and CES1 follow-up values were not supplied in the workbook and were not imputed.

The ALT-paired source blocks further separated general injury markers from etiologic discrimination. Within DO cases, ALT correlated with ASS1 (rho=0.873), ACO1 (0.800), ALDOB (0.798), HPD (0.750), FAH (0.740), OTC (0.701), CPS1 (0.689), GSTA1 (0.668), DMGDH (0.536), CES1 (0.462), FBP1 (0.425) and GLDH (0.353), all at q<0.01. LECT2 and PCK2 were not associated with ALT within DO. The FBP1 correlation was stronger when HV and DO were pooled (rho=0.746), consistent with contribution from between-group separation. Undocumented numeric substitutions for HPD, OTC and PCK2 non-detects in Supplementary Figure 7 were excluded from primary correlation estimates.

Finally, none of the Zone 1, Zone 2 or Zone 3 ssGSEA score comparisons survived FDR control (minimum q=0.182). These scores were therefore retained as descriptive context and were not used to increase candidate priority. Collectively, the public proteomic evidence supports FBP1 as the most etiologically discriminating human DILI phenotype anchor among the 13 source-selected proteins, while ACO1, ASS1, FAH, CPS1, ALDOB and related enzymes define a high-amplitude but largely etiology-agnostic hepatocellular injury module. In the nintedanib analysis, these proteins are distal phenotype readouts; designation of an actionable susceptibility target requires independent nintedanib engagement, human liver cell-state localization, and graded bidirectional functional testing with rescue.
