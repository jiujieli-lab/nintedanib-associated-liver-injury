# Direction of PDGFRA and FLT4 perturbation in nintedanib-associated liver injury

The candidate screen should compare differential receptor engagement. It cannot identify hepatoprotective drugs by selecting the strongest PDGFRA or FLT4 binders. The manuscript prioritizes proteins through unsigned injury-network proximity and reports biochemical engagement by nintedanib; neither establishes whether inhibition of either protein causes, prevents, or merely accompanies injury.

No primary study retrieved in this focused search directly demonstrated that PDGFRA or FLT4 inhibition prevents nintedanib-associated DILI. Absence of a retrieved study is not a systematic-review claim of absence.

## Roles of the 16 compounds

Nintedanib is the index-drug positive reference. Imatinib, crenolanib, avapritinib, and ripretinib offer PDGFRA-directed comparator chemistry; their selectivity, kinase conformation preference, wild-type potency, and liver safety differ. Axitinib, tivozanib, lenvatinib, sunitinib, pazopanib, sorafenib, regorafenib, and ponatinib expand VEGFR/multikinase engagement patterns; labels do not necessarily establish isoform-specific potency, so target-specific biochemical records remain necessary. MAZ51 is a nonclinical FLT4 research comparator, with concentration-dependent selectivity and known activity outside VEGFR3-expressing cells. Pirfenidone and nerandomilast are clinical nonkinase reference compounds, not validated nonbinders to either receptor. The latter two can contextualize pulmonary treatment alternatives but a docking score cannot establish that they are safer for a particular patient.

The matched binding panel contains an informative target-specific weak-binding reference: imatinib PDGFRA Kd = 31 nM and FLT4 Kd > 10,000 nM in the earlier Karaman panel; the Davis panel reproduces these values with duplicate flags. Retain the inequality and do not convert it to an exact 10,000 nM datum. Nintedanib has PDGFRA/FLT4 Kd = 16/95 nM and axitinib 0.51/170 nM in Davis. These are biochemical reference measurements, not comparative clinical doses, selectivity guarantees in liver, or DILI outcomes. [Karaman et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt1358); [Davis et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.1990).

Crenolanib is experimentally supported as a PDGFRA inhibitor, but its primary 2012 paper contains an internal numerical conflict: Results prose assigns PDGFRA Kd 3.2 nM, whereas Table 1 assigns 2.1 nM (the PDGFRB values are interchanged). Preserve this conflict and omit a single definitive Kd from numerical calibration until resolved. Its one directly retrieved parent-molecule ChEMBL PDGFRA record comes from a later review, and is excluded from the primary-source summary. [Heinrich et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-12-0625).

## Interpretation of screening, docking, and dynamics

Screen binding to both receptors using the same verified ligand identities and defined wild-type receptor constructs. Preserve endpoint-specific experimental controls, distinguish mutant/fusion records from wild-type or unspecified constructs, and retain all outliers. Predicted affinities and pose-confidence scores are model outputs. Docking and molecular dynamics can evaluate a plausible binding mode and local stability; they do not establish inhibition, receptor activation, cellular rescue, hepatoprotection, hepatic exposure, or pulmonary efficacy. An ATP-pocket model cannot test ligand-induced extracellular receptor dimerization. A stable complex may represent the unwanted inhibition that a liver-sparing strategy should avoid.

For a putative alternative to nintedanib, weaker FLT4 engagement could be a desirable hypothesis only if pulmonary antifibrotic activity is retained and exposure-matched liver experiments support the hypothesis. For PDGFRA, retain competing hypotheses: reducing pathological stellate-cell signaling may be antifibrotic, whereas changing early regenerative signaling may have different consequences. Do not apply a single sign to the network score or rank dual inhibitors by an assumed hepatic benefit.

## Experimental direction tests for the discussion

Use hepatocytes, stellate cells, liver sinusoidal endothelial cells, lymphatic endothelial cells, and macrophages in separately interpretable or co-culture systems. The manuscript's broad endothelial network cannot substitute for verified lymphatic endothelial identity, and hepatocyte/endothelial/macrophage networks do not directly establish a stellate-cell mechanism. At matched unbound exposure, measure receptor phosphorylation, hepatocyte injury, the prioritized injury proteins, stellate-cell activation, and endothelial/lymphatic function. Compare single and joint genetic suppression with pharmacological inhibition and a matched rescue. Preserve pulmonary antifibrotic readouts in parallel.

PDGF-AA and VEGF-C(Cys156Ser) are signaling probes rather than small-molecule docking candidates. Adding a ligand during kinase-inhibitor occupancy may not rescue signaling; verify phosphorylation before interpreting a failed ligand rescue. Genetic re-expression or a validated inhibitor-resistant construct, a kinase-dead control, and drug washout can help separate receptor dependence from compound off-target toxicity. This is a proposed validation framework, not completed experimental evidence.

## Candidate safety interpretation

Four included oncology inhibitors carry boxed hepatotoxicity warnings in the reviewed US labels: sunitinib, pazopanib, regorafenib, and ponatinib. Several others have hepatic injury warnings or exposure changes in hepatic impairment. Nintedanib and pirfenidone also have DILI warnings. Nerandomilast's distinct mechanism and label profile do not establish head-to-head hepatic superiority. Preserve these limitations in any shortlist, and label every oncology TKI as a mechanistic comparator until actual efficacy, exposure, and safety evidence supports a translational claim.
